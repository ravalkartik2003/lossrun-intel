# Learn Carriers Once, Out-Govern the Vendors

The internal POC wins this bake-off if it does three things on State Fund's own documents that none of the four vendors can demonstrate: beat them on per-field accuracy on a sealed, stratified test set scored against the insurer's ~50,000-document keyed archive; show cell-level lineage, calibrated confidence and a tamper-evident override log that map one-to-one onto what California and NAIC examiners ask for; and prove that a learned carrier format replays deterministically with **zero model calls**, so cost, latency and model-retirement exposure collapse for every repeat carrier. The recommended build is a .NET 10 pipeline on Azure: **Azure AI Content Understanding** for zero-label classification, bundle splitting and layout with per-field confidence and polygon grounding; a versioned **CarrierFormat registry** executed in-process by a deterministic replay engine; **gpt-5.2 on a US Data Zone deployment** used only to synthesize or repair a format spec, inside an agentic loop that validates the spec against the loss run's own printed totals before storing it; **Durable Functions on the Durable Task Scheduler** for orchestration and human-review waits; **Azure SQL ledger and temporal tables** for audit; and client-side what-if analytics in React. The design is the loss-run application of Amazon's CACHE-ED2, which reached **0.99 accuracy with a 100% format-detection hit rate and a 62% token reduction** by compiling LLM reasoning into reusable per-format programs ([CACHE-ED2](https://cdn.amazon.science/7b/83/8b6d627f4346b8df584f7b587922/scipub-approval152129-47230154-cacheed2-compiling-llm-reasoning-into-reusable-extraction-programs-for-document-extraction-at-scale.pdf)). The evaluators' mental model is "buying is safe": insurance executives rank accuracy (71%) and insurance-specific training (45%) far above the ability to build in-house (21%) ([Roots survey](https://www.roots.ai/hubfs/Reports%20and%20Whitepapers/Roots%20-%20State%20of%20AI%20Adoption%20in%20Insurance%202025.pdf)), and MIT's data says internal builds succeed about a third as often as purchased tools ([Fortune/MIT NANDA](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo)), so the proposal must neutralize the ownership question explicitly rather than argue on cost. Among the vendors, only Bevaya has a benchmarked loss-run model (**93.1% macro field accuracy on 346 documents**, vendor-run), none will deploy into State Fund's tenant, and none documents what-if analytics, feature lineage or California cumulative-trauma metrics ([Bevaya benchmarks](https://www.bevaya.ai/benchmarks); [Bevaya security](https://www.bevaya.ai/security)). An eight-week timebox, a sealed 300–500 document blind set, acceptance thresholds filled in per input type, and a 25-minute demo that runs the evaluators' own synthetic documents live from an Azure sandbox turn the developer from a contender into the author of the scoring protocol, and letting the Azure architect co-own and present the production design converts the owner of architecture decisions from judge into sponsor.

## Evaluators buy accuracy on their own documents, then ownership, then everything else

The external evidence an insurer's CIO, head of underwriting and procurement team will have read currently favors buying, and the proposal has to meet that evidence head-on. The MIT NANDA study of roughly 300 deployments found that about **95% of enterprise GenAI pilots show no measurable P&L impact**, that tools bought from specialized vendors succeed about **67% of the time versus roughly 22% for internal builds**, and that the failure driver is integration and a "learning gap," not model quality ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo)). The insurance-specific synthesis of that data says internal builds die because "models decay as document formats drift and models are deprecated, and maintenance depends on a few engineers who can leave," and recommends buying the technology layer while keeping data, judgment and validation accountability in-house ([Faschi](https://www.fabiofaschi.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-in-insurance-who-built-it-who-bought-it-and-who-is-winning)). Capgemini's 2026 P&C report adds that most initiatives stay stuck at proof-of-concept and that **42% of insurers have not measured AI outcomes at all** ([Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/gb-en/insights/research-library/world-property-and-casualty-insurance-report/)). Read together, these say the internal POC loses if it is pitched as "build everything" and wins only if it beats vendors on accuracy-on-our-documents and credibly answers who owns the thing after the pilot. The right frame is the one the literature already accepts: the insurer owns the canonical schema, the validation rule catalog (R-01..R-15), the exception workflow, the audit log and the APIs, and rents commodity models behind one interface, exactly the "headless" pattern a global specialty carrier used when it ran Indico behind its own validation UI ([Indico](https://indicodata.ai/customer-story-global-specialty-headless-implementation/)).

## Content Understanding plus Durable Functions is a production-credible .NET stack

Microsoft's own October 2026 guidance settles the engine choice: "if the schema is business-specific, document variation is high, or labels are unavailable, begin with an Azure Content Understanding custom analyzer," while Document Intelligence stays for stable layouts and on-premises containers ([Foundry blog, Oct 7 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/choosing-azure-document-intelligence-and-content-understanding/)). Content Understanding (CU) is GA on API `2025-11-01` with a stable .NET SDK (`Azure.AI.ContentUnderstanding` 1.1.0), performs schema-described field extraction with no labels, returns per-field confidence and a `D(page,x1,y1,...)` polygon source for every value on rendered inputs, splits multi-document bundles zero-shot through up to 200 `contentCategories` with `enableSegment`, and ingests XLSX/CSV natively at $0.01 per 1,000 pages (one sheet, including hidden sheets, billed as one page) ([CU what's new](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/whats-new); [CU NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/Azure.AI.ContentUnderstanding); [CU elements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/document/elements); [CU classifier](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/concepts/classifier); [CU pricing, Retail API](https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices?api-version=2023-01-01-preview&$filter=contains(productName,%20'Content%20Understanding')%20and%20armRegionName%20eq%20'eastus')). Microsoft's October 13 tax-form benchmark puts CU 2.0 preview at **99.32% F1 versus 96.71% for Document Intelligence at roughly DI's latency**, and CU is where Microsoft says its investment is going ([Foundry blog, Oct 13 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/more-than-a-schema-content-understanding-prebuilt-analyzers/)). Document Intelligence, by contrast, has had no new API version since `2024-11-30`, its .NET SDK has not shipped a release since December 2024, its custom neural model needs five labeled samples per template, and its legacy "Query Pages" meter bills **$200 per 1,000 pages**, twenty times the Layout rate, a trap for anyone who tries `queryFields` as a cheap zero-shot extractor ([DI model overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/model-overview?view=doc-intel-4.0.0); [DI NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/Azure.AI.DocumentIntelligence); [DI pricing, Retail API](https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices?api-version=2023-01-01-preview&$filter=contains(productName,%20'Document%20Intelligence')%20and%20armRegionName%20eq%20'eastus')). Keep DI only as a second-opinion OCR engine and as the answer if an evaluator asks about an air-gapped container.

The recommended pipeline runs as one Durable Functions orchestration per upload. A .NET pre-processing activity sniffs file types, unlocks password-protected PDFs with PdfPig's `ParsingOptions.Password` against a configured password list (neither CU nor DI opens locked PDFs: "you must remove the lock before submission"), and parses XLSX/XLS/XLSB/CSV with ExcelDataReader, which supports workbook passwords, capturing sheet and cell addresses as lineage because neither Azure service returns coordinates for spreadsheets ([DI input requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/read?view=doc-intel-4.0.0); [PdfPig](https://www.nuget.org/packages/PdfPig); [ExcelDataReader](https://www.nuget.org/packages/ExcelDataReader); [CU elements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/document/elements)). A CU classifier call segments the bundle into `LossRun`, `ExperienceModWorksheet`, `Payroll`, `Other` page ranges with a confidence per segment, which is Use Case 1's "mixed document intake and auto-classification" scenario solved without training data. Each loss-run segment becomes a sub-orchestration that calls CU `prebuilt-layout` (Standard meter, **$5 per 1,000 pages**; Basic Read is $1) to get markdown, tables, cells, word confidence and polygons, then hands that document graph to the format-memory router described in the next section. Validation rules run on every document, including replay hits; failures land in a review queue implemented with `WaitForExternalEventAsync` raced against a durable timer, during which "no charges accrue while an orchestrator awaits an external event" on the Consumption plan ([Durable external events](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-functions/durable/durable-functions-external-events)). The Durable Task Scheduler is GA and Microsoft's recommended backend, bills **$0.003 per action** on Consumption, ships an RBAC-secured dashboard that doubles as a free observability demo, and caps payloads at 1 MB, so every CU/DI JSON result must live in Blob with only URIs passed through the orchestration ([DTS overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-functions/durable/durable-task-scheduler/durable-task-scheduler); [DTS billing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-functions/durable/durable-task-scheduler/durable-task-scheduler-billing)). Host it on the Flex Consumption plan with the .NET isolated worker (the in-process model reaches end of support on 10 November 2026), and remember that HTTP triggers must answer within 230 seconds, which forces the API shape to "accept upload, return a job id, stream results as steps complete" ([Flex Consumption](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-functions/flex-consumption-plan); [Functions scale](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-functions/functions-scale)). This is the same shape as Microsoft's own June 2026 reference architecture for document classification, which pairs Durable Functions with Document Intelligence, Cosmos DB and AI Search ([Architecture Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/ai-ml/architecture/automate-document-classification-durable-functions)).

For the model layer, use **gpt-5.2 on a Data Zone Standard (US) deployment** as the learn-path model: it is Microsoft's recommended completion model for CU, costs **$1.925 input / $15.40 output per million tokens** in the US data zone, accepts PDFs through the Responses API as text plus a rendered page image, supports strict structured outputs, and retires on 8 June 2027 ([CU models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/concepts/models-deployments); [gpt-5.2 Retail API](https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices?api-version=2023-01-01-preview&$filter=productName%20eq%20'Azure%20OpenAI%20GPT5'%20and%20armRegionName%20eq%20'eastus2'%20and%20contains(skuName,%20'5.2')); [Responses API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/responses); [Model retirement schedule](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/model-retirement-schedule)). Use gpt-5.4-mini (**$0.825 / $4.95** Data Zone) for header-delta repairs and classification, and route archive backfills through Global Batch at 50% off ([gpt-5.4-mini Retail API](https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices?api-version=2023-01-01-preview&$filter=productName%20eq%20'Azure%20OpenAI%20GPT5'%20and%20armRegionName%20eq%20'eastus2'%20and%20contains(skuName,%20'5.4%20mini')); [Batch](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/batch)). Do not build anything new on the GPT-4.1 family, which retires on 14 April 2027 and which CU Studio already warns is being dropped. Parameterize the model name everywhere: Azure gives GA models an 18-month lifecycle with 60 days' retirement notice, and that calendar is the single most-cited reason internal builds decay ([Model retirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/openai/concepts/model-retirements)). Claude Opus, Sonnet and Fable models are available in Foundry with Data Zone Standard in eight US regions and are a legitimate learn-path alternative, but CU's supported completion list is GPT-only, so the CU analyzer itself must run on gpt-5.x ([Partner models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/foundry-models/concepts/models-from-partners)).

The data layer should be Azure SQL as the system of record, with Blob as the document store. Blob version-level WORM and legal hold are assessed for SEC 17a-4(f), FINRA 4511 and CFTC 1.31 at no extra capacity charge ($0.0184/GB-month Hot LRS), which makes the original uploads and raw extraction JSON immutable by policy ([Immutable storage](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/immutable-storage-overview)). Azure SQL ledger tables, GA since 2022, block UPDATE and DELETE at the engine for append-only tables and chain SHA-256 Merkle-tree digests to immutable Blob or Azure Confidential Ledger every 30 seconds, giving cryptographic proof that the override log was not altered ([Ledger overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/security/ledger/ledger-overview); [Digest management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/security/ledger/ledger-digest-management)). Cosmos DB serverless at $0.25 per million RUs is a fine store for document and lineage JSON, but its only change-feed mode that captures every intermediate version is still preview and bounded by the continuous-backup window, which makes it the weaker audit store ([Cosmos serverless](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cosmos-db/serverless); [Change feed processor](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cosmos-db/nosql/change-feed-processor)). For the v2 chat assistant, AI Search Basic costs about $74 a month and its Content Understanding skill went GA in REST `2026-04-01`; index the already-extracted markdown from Blob rather than re-running CU through the skill ([AI Search CU skill](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/search/cognitive-search-skill-content-understanding)).

## Format memory compiles each carrier into a versioned, replayable extractor

The design constraint, "learn each carrier's format and vocabulary once, replay deterministically, model as fallback," is the best-evidenced pattern in the 2023–2026 extraction literature, and the evidence should be cited in the proposal because it answers the "why not just call GPT on every page" question evaluators will raise. Stanford's Evaporate showed LLM-synthesized extraction functions use about **110x fewer tokens** than per-document extraction and, when ensembled, beat direct extraction by 10 F1 points, with the warning that more than 40% of single generated functions score below 25 F1, so verification is mandatory ([Evaporate, PVLDB](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p92-arora.pdf)). MIT's SEED framed the LLM as a compiler and answered 60–100% of records with no runtime model call ([SEED](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00749v3)). Amazon's CACHE-ED2, presented at ICML 2026, moved from raw generated code to a constrained DSL (strategies, candidates, disambiguation, transforms, validation), had a ReAct agent execute its program against the page and compare before storing, and on 5,000 real invoices from five vendors went from **0.81 (string-fingerprint version) to 0.99 accuracy with a 1.0 extraction rate**, with cache hits costing zero LLM calls and cumulative cost beating the baseline after about 50 documents ([CACHE-ED2](https://cdn.amazon.science/7b/83/8b6d627f4346b8df584f7b587922/scipub-approval152129-47230154-cacheed2-compiling-llm-reasoning-into-reusable-extraction-programs-for-document-extraction-at-scale.pdf)). Production practice agrees: Sensible's largest loss-run customer scaled from about 50 to roughly 300 deterministic per-carrier templates and found deterministic methods gave the precision LLMs could not on dense loss runs, and even a template-free vendor concedes templates "may win on accuracy" for stable high-volume suppliers ([Sensible](https://sensible.so/blog/how-two-insurance-companies-expedited-underwriting-with-automated-loss-run-processing); [Hypatos](https://www.hypatos.ai/knowledge-base/template-free-document-extraction-enterprise)).

Fingerprinting decides whether the whole design pays off, because CACHE-ED2's 62% token saving versus 31% for its predecessor was driven almost entirely by the format-detection hit rate. A naive OCR-string fingerprint averaged only a **47% hit rate** on real vendor documents because trivial variance ("AMAZON SITE" vs "AMAZON FC SITE") caused misses, whereas a learned layout-aware first-page embedding compared to per-format centroids at **cosine threshold 0.95** hit 100% ([CACHE-ED2](https://cdn.amazon.science/7b/83/8b6d627f4346b8df584f7b587922/scipub-approval152129-47230154-cacheed2-compiling-llm-reasoning-into-reusable-extraction-programs-for-document-extraction-at-scale.pdf)). A 2025 clustering study adds the caveat that vision-only embeddings "fell sharply" under scan noise while text-based embeddings held up, which argues for fingerprinting on Layout-extracted text rather than pixels ([arXiv 2506.12116](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12116)). The practical two-tier design is a cheap deterministic key first (for XLSX/CSV, a hash of the normalized header tuple plus sheet count; for PDFs, a hash of the Layout `title`/`pageHeader` paragraphs plus the first table's column-header cells) and a text-embedding-3-large vector over the first page's Layout markdown compared to per-carrier-format centroids second. Sensible's declarative `fingerprint` block, a list of text tests that must all pass before a config activates, is the production precedent for the deterministic tier ([Sensible fingerprints](https://www.sensible.so/blog/how-to-extract-data-from-insurance-declaration-pages-with-sensible)).

The artifact that gets learned is not free-form code but a versioned declarative spec, which can be validated, diffed and executed in-process in .NET without sandboxing generated code. Each `CarrierFormat` record carries the fingerprint, file kind, a table locator (sheet or table index, header row, data start row, stop rule for subtotal and continuation rows), a column map from source header to canonical field with a normalizer and enum map, derived rules such as `total_incurred = paid_total + reserve_total`, validation rules such as `sum(paid_total) == reportTotalPaid ± 1.00`, per-field success counters, and lineage of which model and prompt version learned it and who reviewed it. Versions are never mutated: a re-learn creates `formatId.version+1`, the old version stays attached to every document it processed, and every extracted claim row carries its `formatVersion`, which is the schema-versioning practice data engineering already uses for drift ([Estuary](https://estuary.dev/blog/schema-drift/)). CACHE-ED2's per-field "document-processed score" is worth copying verbatim: a field that has reconciled 78 times makes the system skeptical of a single contradicting human correction, which protects the registry from one-off anomalies ([CACHE-ED2](https://cdn.amazon.science/7b/83/8b6d627f4346b8df584f7b587922/scipub-approval152129-47230154-cacheed2-compiling-llm-reasoning-into-reusable-extraction-programs-for-document-extraction-at-scale.pdf)).

The learn path is where the model earns its keep, and the agentic verification loop is the difference between 0.81 and 0.99. On a fingerprint miss, gpt-5.2 receives the Layout markdown of the first two or three pages plus a rendered page image and the glossary, and emits the spec through strict structured outputs; the orchestrator executes the spec on the same document, runs the rule catalog, and feeds any failure (unmapped headers, totals that do not tie, dates that do not parse) back for up to N iterations before persisting. Loss runs give this loop an unusually strong free oracle: the document's own printed subtotals and grand totals. Heron Data checks "totals and ratios against per-claim sums and carrier conventions," Outmarket "compares extracted totals with the totals stated in the document," and Concirrus validates "totals, calculations and claim details," so reconciliation is already the universal validator in the market ([Heron Data](https://www.herondata.io/action-guides/parse-for-loss-runs); [Outmarket](https://outmarket.ai/blog/outmarket-ai-launches-ai-powered-loss-run-extraction-and-analysis); [Concirrus](https://concirrus.ai/loss-run-intelligence/)). CACHE-ED2's rule that validation "runs even on cache hits" and is "fully deterministic, LLM-free" should be adopted as a non-negotiable. When the spec synthesis cannot represent a layout (narrative-style runs, multi-line claim blocks), the fallback is a CU custom analyzer with the canonical loss-run schema, which returns per-field confidence and polygons without any labels and whose descriptions act as mini-prompts carrying alias lists ("also labeled 'Date of Loss' or 'DOL'") ([CU best practices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/concepts/best-practices)). In the `2026-06-01-preview`, adding one labeled sample per carrier template distills patterns into the analyzer and lowers per-call tokens, which is a cheap later optimization once the schema stabilizes ([Analyzer improvement](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/document/analyzer-improvement)).

The routing policy that falls out of this has five tiers, and the proposal should present it as a table because it is the operational definition of "model as fallback." A fingerprint hit with an exact header signature goes to deterministic replay with no model. Replay that passes every validator is auto-accepted. Replay with a few unmapped headers or a single failed validator goes to a "delta" call on gpt-5.4-mini with only the unknown headers, sample rows and the glossary, then re-validates. A fingerprint miss, a failed delta, low mean OCR word confidence, or handwritten pages goes to the full learn path. Anything still failing goes to the review queue with page and cell citations, and reviewer corrections are converted into spec edits and a new format version. Spreadsheets almost never need the model: a deterministic header-row detector (first row whose cells are mostly strings that resolve against the glossary, followed by a row with numeric or date cells) plus the glossary resolves most exports, and the model is called only when header resolution falls below roughly 80% or two stacked header rows ("Indemnity" over "Paid / Reserve / Incurred") score similarly. Azure's Layout explicitly does not do table analysis on XLSX, so native parsing is the only deterministic path for Excel anyway ([DI Layout](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/layout?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)).

The "glossary" the user describes is a canonical schema of roughly 30 fields plus per-carrier vocabulary, and it can be bootstrapped without labels from public standards and one LLM expansion pass. Field semantics and code sets are standardized even though loss-run layouts are not: the WCIRB Unit Statistical Reporting handbook defines claim number as the sole matching key, status codes (0 open, 1 closed), injury codes (01 death through 06 medical-only), and incurred as "paid plus outstanding reserves as of the valuation date"; the WCIO Injury Description Tables supply the two-digit Part of Body, Nature and Cause codes that California's WCIS uses; and the actuarial identity incurred = paid + case reserve is fixed ([WCIRB USR handbook](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/2023_sept_data_reporting_handbook-usr.pdf); [WCIO Part table](http://laborcommission.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WCIO_Part_Table.pdf); [CA WCIS](https://dir.ca.gov/dwc/wcis/WCIS_tables/Tables1-3/WCIS_Reports-Table1-3.html); [Milliman glossary](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/a-beginners-guide-to-the-casualty-actuarial-language)). The header-synonym layer is what no standard publishes, and the schema-matching literature shows LLM synonym expansion is the single largest contributor to matching accuracy: SCHEMORA's hit-rate@5 drops from 80.4% to 67.3% when name expansion is removed, and its retrieve-then-rerank design (BM25 plus embeddings over an LLM-enriched glossary, small LLM reranking only ambiguous ties) resolves most headers with no model call ([SCHEMORA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14376v1); [Magneto](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08194)). Seed each canonical field with the aliases observed on real documents (claim number appears as "Claim #," "ClaimNo/Sts," "Commission claim number," "File #"; date of injury as "DOI," "DOL," "Date of Accident," "Loss Date"; reserves as "O/S," "Outstanding," "Case Reserves," "Remaining"), ask gpt-5.2 once to expand each with abbreviations and carrier-style variants while avoiding the canonical wording, and then grow the glossary from every confirmed mapping with its carrier provenance. Code-set mapping must be tagged per code system, because Minnesota uses three-digit codes and carriers often print free text, and free-text-to-WCIO mapping must be scored separately from extraction ([Minnesota coding guide](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/wc_injury_coding_tcm36-209466.pdf)).

Confidence has to be calibrated, not trusted, and this is a place to be explicit with evaluators. Microsoft's own 2024 benchmark found logprob-based "confidence" above 99% where accuracy was 67–79% ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azureforisvandstartupstechnicalblog/evaluating-the-quality-of-ai-document-data-extraction-with-small-and-large-langu/4157719)); a 2026 study found native verbalized confidence has AUROC of only 0.54–0.74 while a decomposed perception-plus-layout-plus-validation score reaches 0.90–0.99 and lets **49–72% of fields auto-approve at under 10% error** ([arXiv 2609.20110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20110)); and a second study showed fused multi-signal scores with isotonic recalibration reach 99.1% automated accuracy at 80% coverage ([arXiv 2606.24420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24420)). For loss runs the arithmetic reconciliation is the strongest signal and costs nothing, so the proposal's confidence model is: validator outcomes first, CU per-field confidence and OCR word confidence second, logprobs as a weak feature only, with thresholds set per field criticality and re-tuned whenever a model or analyzer changes, which Microsoft's October 2026 calibration guidance also requires ([Microsoft Foundry blog, Oct 14 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/content-understanding-confidence-grounding-hitl/)).

One honest point belongs in the TCO slide: at list prices the per-page Layout meter, not the LLM, dominates per-document cost once extraction is deterministic. A 12-page document on Document Intelligence Layout costs about $0.13 with a small-model mapping call and about $0.12 without one, so the "minimize LLM calls" design mainly buys determinism, sub-second replay latency, auditability and insulation from model retirements rather than dollars ([Azure DI pricing reprint](https://parsli.co/compare/azure-document-intelligence)). The cost case still closes: with CU Read at $1 per 1,000 pages or native XLSX parsing, the replay floor is a few cents or effectively zero, and annual cloud consumption for 50,000 documents lands in the low five figures.

## A sealed 300–500 document set scored against the keyed archive sets the thresholds

"No labelled training data" is true for training and false for evaluation, and the distinction is the POC's biggest hidden asset. The ~50,000 archived loss runs whose values were keyed by humans are silver labels that, aligned on (normalized claim number, date of injury, policy number or period, carrier), score every contender on identical terms, and no vendor's public process asks for ground truth up front, so the build side controls the only scoring data that exists ([Bevaya implementation](https://www.bevaya.ai/implementation)). Two corrections keep that data honest. First, keyed data is itself imperfect: a 2024 study found reference sets only 86–95% accurate and human reviewers agreeing only 71–86% of the time, so a 10% sub-sample must be double-keyed by an underwriting assistant and the measured keying error reported as the ground-truth margin ([KDAN](https://www.kdan.com/blog/measure-audit-intelligent-document-processing-accuracy)). Second, keyed dollar values are a valuation snapshot; only documents whose printed "as of" date matches the keyed snapshot date may serve as labels for paid, reserve and incurred, while claim number, DOI, injury descriptors and policy period are stable across valuations and can be used from every aligned record. Adjudicate only disagreements between the extractor, the keyed record and a second extraction path, which is how ExtractBench built its truth ("cross-model agreement with human review of disagreements") and how Microsoft built its 22,000-field calibration set from production documents ([ExtractBench](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/introducing-extractbench); [Microsoft Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/content-understanding-confidence-grounding-hitl/)). Few-shot LLM silver labels alone are not acceptable as truth; one study measured about **15.7% mean absolute error** when GPT-4 labels stood in for human ones, versus about 2% for ensemble-disagreement scores ([GEM 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.gem-1.5.pdf)). Synthetic loss runs generated with known truth, aged with scan artifacts in the AWS SEED style, are the right tool for edge-case coverage (reopened claims, multiple policy numbers, non-CA losses, catastrophic outliers), but their results must be reported separately and never blended into the headline number ([AWS SEED](https://awslabs.github.io/synthetically_engineered_evaluation_data/)).

Sample sizes are arithmetic, and the proposal should show the arithmetic. At worst-case 50% accuracy, ±5% at 95% confidence needs 400 items and ±3% needs about 1,067; at an expected 95% accuracy, ±2% needs about 456 field values and ±1% about 1,825 ([Sample size determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_size_determination)). But fields cluster within documents, and an unadjusted interval with ~30 fields per document covered the true value only about 65% of the time in one analysis, so report Agresti-Coull intervals with a design-effect correction or a document-level bootstrap, and plan on roughly **547 positives for a ±2.5% half-width at 90% true accuracy** ([arXiv 2606.26422](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26422)). Practitioner protocols converge on the same scale: 200–500 stratified documents, ground truth and metrics frozen before testing, 20% deliberate edge cases mixed into normal batches so entrants cannot tell which are stress tests, identical samples and scoring for every contender, a 4–8 week timebox, and a pre-agreed weighted scorecard ([Adlib](https://www.adlibsoftware.com/news/how-to-evaluate-ai-document-automation-platform); [AlterSquare](https://altersquare.io/blog/document-ai-audit-template-evaluate-vendor-pipeline-edge-cases)). The protocol to propose to the architect and procurement is therefore: the actuarial or underwriting team holds a sealed set of 300–500 documents stratified by input type (native PDF, XLSX/CSV, scan), by carrier template (top carriers plus a long-tail bucket) and by the requirement document's edge cases; a separate ~100-document tuning set goes to every entrant in advance; every entrant receives the same canonical schema and the same published scoring script; and every entrant must return a confidence per field so error-coverage-at-review-rate curves can be compared. The developer never sees the blind set until scoring day. This plays to the build side's strengths because traceability to the source cell, reviewer minutes per exception, and customizable mapping tables are scored items that the RFP guidance already recommends weighting ([invoicedataextraction RFP checklist](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/intelligent-document-processing-rfp-checklist); [idp-software evaluation guide](https://idp-software.com/guides/idp-vendor-evaluation/)).

Metric definitions matter as much as thresholds, because token-overlap scorers hide the errors that matter. A $12,345.00 read as $12,354.00 scores well on token F1, so the comparator must be typed: dates to ISO, currency to Decimal with parentheses as negatives, claim numbers normalized for whitespace, leading zeros and punctuation, codes compared after WCIO mapping, and long free text fuzzy-matched only above a high similarity, which is RealDocBench's convention ([RealDocBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07401)). Report per-field accuracy, per-claim joint pass (all required fields right, the number that runs 5–17 points below per-field), record-level table match keyed by headers, classification precision and recall, reconciliation pass rate, STP rate at the chosen threshold, p50/p95 latency, cost per document, and error types split into missing, wrong and wrong-but-plausible, "the most dangerous" class ([ParseBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08538); [LandingAI](https://landing.ai/geo-content-strategy/how-to-test-document-extraction-accuracy-before-choosing-a-vendor)). Implement the comparator as a Foundry custom code evaluator so every run is logged with the git SHA and model version and can be rerun in CI on every prompt, rule or model change ([Foundry custom evaluators](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-my/Azure/foundry/concepts/evaluation-evaluators/custom-evaluators)). A document whose printed totals do not themselves sum must go to a separate "source inconsistent" bucket so the extractor is not penalized for carrier errors, which then become underwriting signal.

The thresholds below fill the requirement document's placeholders and are calibrated to the 2025–2026 benchmark picture: on mixed real-world business documents the best systems score roughly **90–96% per field** (RealDocBench 86–96% for top systems, ExtractBench value-F1 87–96%), born-digital PDFs run 97–99.5%, scans below 150 DPI lose 12–25 points, and straight-through rates run 85–92% for controlled sources and 65–80% for uncontrolled ones ([RealDocBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07401); [ExtractBench](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/introducing-extractbench); [DigiParser](https://digiparser.com/statistics/ocr-accuracy-by-document-type); [LlamaIndex OCR guide](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/ocr-automation)). Loss runs are easier than mortgage packets (fixed tabular layouts, short alphanumeric keys, printed totals), so the targets sit at the top of those ranges for native inputs. Latency has no published Azure figure for DI Layout, CU analysis or gpt-5.x throughput, so the latency thresholds are proposals to be fixed after week-two measurement; the only external anchors are Bevaya's 20.9 seconds per loss run and Microsoft's 22–135 second benchmark latencies for frontier extraction ([Bevaya benchmarks](https://www.bevaya.ai/benchmarks); [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azureforisvandstartupstechnicalblog/evaluating-the-quality-of-ai-document-data-extraction-with-small-and-large-langu/4157719)).

| Requirement placeholder | Proposed threshold (blind set, reported by input type and seen/unseen template, with 95% CI) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Extraction accuracy, key fields | Claim # and DOI ≥98% native PDF/XLSX, ≥95% scans ≥300 DPI, ≥90% faxes/<200 DPI; indemnity/medical paid and reserved ≥97% native, ≥93% scans; nature/cause/body ≥95% when codes are printed, ≥85% top-1 WCIO mapping from free text with an explicit NOC bucket | Benchmark ranges above; code sets are closed but free text is not |
| Claim-level completeness (all required fields present and reconciled) | ≥90% native, ≥80% scans | Per-question rates run 5–17 points below per-field |
| Auto-classification | Precision ≥97%, recall ≥95% for loss run vs other, WC vs other line, CA vs non-CA, bundle split boundaries | 97%+ page-level classification observed in an insurance CU deployment |
| Data-quality validation first pass | ≥85% of internally consistent documents pass all hard rules without edits | Healthy pipelines run 5–10% exceptions |
| Quote success rate (no human touch) | ≥80% overall, with ≥85% on recurring templates and ≥65% on first-seen templates reported separately | STP 85–92% controlled / 65–80% uncontrolled |
| End-to-end latency, ≤15 MB upload | p95 time-to-first-claim ≤15 s; p95 time-to-quote ≤60 s for known formats, ≤120 s when the learn path runs | No published Azure figures; Bevaya 20.9 s/doc; must be measured, API is asynchronous |
| End-to-end latency, 50 MB mixed batch | p95 time-to-complete ≤5 min with progressive per-document results | CU 300-page/200 MB async limit; quota-bound |

| UC2 p95 interactive action | ≤1.0 s server round-trip for account load and override save; ≤200 ms Interaction-to-Next-Paint for slider and drill-down | Client-side recomputation; INP "good" band |
| Audit completeness | 100% by construction, verified by a replay test | Ledger tables plus temporal history reconciliation |

The validation rule catalog is where the build side is more insurance-specific than any vendor's model, and it should be presented as such. Statistical-plan edits already encode the hard rules: "paid indemnity cannot be greater than incurred indemnity," "no duplicate claim numbers allowed," "total claim amount for loss > $2,000 must have a claim number," and "incurred indemnity amount must be zero for injury code of 6 or 7" ([NCRB USR edits](https://www.ncrb.org/Portals/0/ncrb/workers%20comp%20services/Data%20Reporting/USR%20Edits%20as%20of%20032021.pdf)). Add DOI not after the valuation date or the report date, DOI inside a policy period on the run, incurred = paid + reserve within rounding, closed implies zero reserves (soft where a carrier is known to show residual reserves), no negatives outside recovery columns, per-period subtotals and open/closed counts equal to extracted rows, and a "no losses" statement implying zero rows. Soft rules cover reopened claims (status "R," or closed in an earlier valuation and open with reserves later), missing policy years, stale valuation dates against State Fund's own requirement that loss runs be "valued within 100 days of the policy inception date," reporting lag, large-loss flags, non-CA jurisdiction and non-WC lines ([State Fund loss run requirements](https://www.statefundca.com/broker/how-to-submit-a-quote/loss-run-information/)). Mapping the existing R-01..R-15 catalog onto this list, and showing which rule fired on which cell in the demo, is a scored traceability item vendors cannot match.

## Client-side what-if and ledger-backed audit are the underwriter analytics edge

Use Case 2 is where no vendor competes: public materials from Bevaya, Convr, Nomad, Federato, Kalepa, Groundspeed and Concirrus describe triage flags, loss summaries and "source-linked answers," but none documents what-if sliders, per-field confidence display, or a lineage view from source cell to feature ([Federato](https://www.federato.ai); [Kalepa](https://kalepa.com/solution); [Celent: Groundspeed](https://celent.com/vendormatch/discovery/solutions/988909439); [Nomad](https://www.nomad-data.com/)). The closest citable UI patterns are Microsoft's MIT-licensed content-processing accelerator, which pairs Content Understanding with GPT-5.1 vision, scores confidence from OCR and log-probability signals, and gives reviewers side-by-side source comparison with annotations, and IDP vendors' hover-to-highlight bounding boxes, which in one documented case caught a wrong-column extraction that a 0.97 confidence score had passed ([content-processing accelerator](https://github.com/microsoft/content-processing-solution-accelerator); [Nutrient](https://www.nutrient.io/blog/document-extraction-confidence-scores/)). A thin but complete slice, three or four canned views, one what-if, click-through lineage, and an override log, is enough to differentiate.

The sub-second interaction requirement decides the visualization architecture, and it decides against Power BI for the interactive views. A per-account dataset of 1,000–5,000 claims fits in browser memory (TanStack Table is documented for tens of thousands of client-side rows), so every slider (reserves ±10%, exclude catastrophes, trend factor) recomputes all catalog metrics in a web worker in milliseconds, well inside the 200 ms Interaction-to-Next-Paint "good" band, with zero server round-trips ([TanStack Table](https://tanstack.com/table/latest/docs/guide/pagination); [web.dev INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp)). Power BI Embedded's own documentation works against the requirement: "applying filters, bookmarks, or slicers at embed time bypasses the cache," every slicer move is a capacity round-trip, the smallest SKUs throttle interactive requests under sustained overload, a what-if parameter is limited to 1,000 values, and no documented host-app API sets a what-if parameter, with the only community thread on the question recording no solution ([Embedded performance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/Power-Bi/developer/embedded/embedded-performance-best-practices); [What-if parameters](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/transform-model/desktop-what-if); [Fabric community thread](https://community.fabric.microsoft.com/t5/Service/Alternative-Ways-to-Update-What-If-Parameter-in-Embedded-Report/td-p/4023544)). The credible hybrid is custom React analytics (Apache ECharts on canvas for dense scatter and large-loss plots, Recharts with its default accessibility layer for small charts, TanStack Table for claim drill-down) for interactive work, plus one Power BI report on an **F2 capacity at about $0.36 per hour** with app-owns-data embedding and no per-viewer licensing for the "official" underwriting report package, which reuses the client's existing Power BI skills ([ECharts](https://github.com/apache/echarts); [Recharts accessibility](https://github.com/recharts/recharts/wiki/Recharts-and-accessibility); [Fabric capacities](https://community.fabric.microsoft.com/blog/fbc_fabricupdatesblogs/announcing-microsoft-fabric-capacities-are-available-for-purchase/5172353); [Fabric licenses](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/enterprise/licenses)). Azure Managed Grafana (Entra-only, no documented embed) and Fabric Embed (preview, Real-Time Dashboards only, delegated tokens only) are excluded with one line each ([Fabric Embed](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/embed/what-is-fabric-embed)). Consistency between what-if and "official" numbers is proven by running one metric library on both sides: the server computes the as-reported baseline, persists it as a versioned feature snapshot behind `HybridCache` with tag invalidation per account, and golden-file tests assert the client and server produce identical values on the reference set, which doubles as the evidence for the insight-accuracy criterion ([HybridCache](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/aspnet/core/performance/caching/hybrid)).

Lineage should be modeled as one record per extracted value stored beside the claim field, not as a separate graph, so "click value, highlight source" is a single lookup. Both Azure services emit exactly the needed pieces, page number, four-point polygon in inches for PDFs or pixels for images, character spans and confidence, but only for rendered files, so spreadsheet lineage records sheet name and A1 cell address from the workbook and highlights the cell in a rendered grid rather than a rasterized sheet ([CU get-result](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/rest/api/contentunderstanding/content-analyzers/get-result?view=rest-contentunderstanding-2025-11-01); [DI analyze response](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/concept/analyze-document-response)). Describe the chain in W3C PROV vocabulary (entity, activity, wasDerivedFrom) with OpenLineage's column-level transformation types (IDENTITY, TRANSFORMATION, AGGREGATION) in the governance deliverable; both are recognized standards, and neither requires the POC to emit full OpenLineage events ([PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); [OpenLineage column lineage](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/facets/dataset-facets/column_lineage_facet/)). For click-to-highlight, inches times 72 gives PDF points, and react-pdf-highlighter's viewport-independent `ScaledPosition` renders the box over PDF.js ([react-pdf-highlighter](https://github.com/agentcooper/react-pdf-highlighter)).

The "100% of edits tracked" criterion is provable live rather than asserted. Model the resolved claim field as a temporal table, the override as an append-only ledger row with old value, new value, required rationale, user, role, timestamp, document version and catalog version, and every mutation (uploads, re-extractions, exports, quote pushes, permission changes) as a ledger audit event, all written in one SQL transaction by a single command handler that is the only code path allowed to touch the claim field. The demo then shows a reconciliation query (history versions minus one per field equals override rows), a live `UPDATE` on the audit table failing with the ledger error, and `sp_verify_database_ledger` validating against the latest digest in immutable Blob ([Append-only ledger tables](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/security/ledger/ledger-how-to-append-only-ledger-tables); [EF Core temporal tables](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/ef/core/providers/sql-server/temporal-tables)). As-reported versus as-adjusted is two feature snapshots, one from extracted values and one from resolved values, with the override list explaining the difference, and temporal `AS OF` answers "what did the underwriter see at quote time." This record is a superset of the fields Bevaya documents for its reviewer log, which is the comparison to put on the slide ([Bevaya HITL](https://www.bevaya.ai/platform/human-in-the-loop)).

Permissions, integration and performance proof follow Azure's documented patterns. Define four Entra app roles (Applicant, Underwriter, UWManager, Admin) assigned to security groups so the `roles` claim stays tiny and avoids the 200-group JWT overage, enforce them with ASP.NET Core policy and resource-based authorization (account ownership, override rights, export rights), put self-serve applicants in an Entra External ID tenant whose first 50,000 monthly active users are free, and export Entra's own audit log of role and group changes, which records old and new values and "can't be changed or deleted," to the same Log Analytics sink as the application audit ([App roles](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/howto-add-app-roles-in-apps); [Group claims](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/develop/configure-tokens-group-claims-app-roles); [External ID pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/pricing/microsoft-entra-external-id/); [Entra audit logs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/monitoring-health/concept-audit-logs)). For the quote-management sandbox, use Service Bus (sessions, duplicate detection, dead-letter) for commands such as annotations, flags and follow-up assignments, Event Grid CloudEvents webhooks with 24-hour retries and Blob dead-lettering for notifications, and a transactional outbox so every push is both audited and delivered at least once with an idempotency key; the acceptance evidence is an empty dead-letter queue and the annotation ids visible in the sandbox ([Messaging options](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/guide/technology-choices/messaging); [Event Grid retry](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/event-grid/delivery-and-retry); [Transactional outbox](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/best-practices/transactional-outbox-cosmos)). Shape the adapter on Guidewire's inbound Cloud API plus outbound App Events so it maps onto Guidewire, Duck Creek or Majesco later ([Guidewire App Events](https://www.guidewire.com/resources/blog/technology/simplify-event-driven-integrations-on-guidewire-cloud-with-app-events)). Render the pricing memo as DOCX with the MIT Open XML SDK, because QuestPDF's free Community license explicitly excludes "public-sector bodies and government agencies," which covers State Fund ([QuestPDF license](https://www.questpdf.com/license/guide.html); [Open XML SDK](https://github.com/dotnet/Open-XML-SDK)). Prove performance with Azure Load Testing, where a single engine supports 250 JMeter threads and pass/fail criteria such as `p95(response_time_ms) < 1000` are first-class, for the API sequence, and with Application Insights `startTrackEvent/stopTrackEvent` around slider-to-render in 25 parallel Playwright browsers for the client side; the Well-Architected guidance to set thresholds per layer and compare against a baseline is the template for the "performance test results" deliverable ([Azure Load Testing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/load-testing/overview-what-is-azure-load-testing); [Fail criteria](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/load-testing/how-to-define-test-criteria); [App Insights JS SDK](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/app/javascript-sdk); [WAF performance testing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/performance-efficiency/performance-test)).

The analytics catalog should be a versioned YAML modeled on dbt MetricFlow (name, type simple/ratio/derived, expression, numerator and denominator, filters, owner), extended with valuation date, development caveats, confidence dependencies, reference-set tolerance and California notes, so the same records generate the server metric functions, the client what-if definitions and the sign-off document ([dbt metrics](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/metrics-overview)). California data dictates which metrics come first. Cumulative trauma rose from 13% of indemnity claims in 2012 to **26.6% in preliminary 2024 and 31.6% in 2025**, more than 90% of recent CT claims are litigated, about 60% are filed after employment ends, 22% remain open after five years versus 12% for non-CT, and the WCIRB projects a **127% combined ratio for accident year 2025**, the highest in more than twenty years ([Business Insurance, July 2026](https://www.businessinsurance.com/surging-cumulative-trauma-claims-driving-frequency-costs/); [Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/california-cumulative-trauma-claims-surge-now-represent-a-quarter-of-workers-comp-pure-premium/); [Business Insurance, Oct 2026](https://www.businessinsurance.com/california-comp-report-signals-pressure-for-higher-employer-costs/)). A CA underwriter therefore reads a loss run for CT share, litigation rate, post-termination filings and open-claim reserves before anything else, and CT signals are derivable from the canonical schema (nature codes 60–83 or "cumulative" text, cause 97/98, type-of-loss 02/03, multi-body-part codes, DOI at or after last day worked, zero medical with indemnity or ALAE paid). Experience-rating inputs are also canonical-schema outputs: California's plan uses per-claim indemnity plus medical incurred capped at the Maximum Loss Value, a $250 per-claim deduction, size-based primary thresholds from $4,500 to $75,000, and net-of-subrogation treatment, which is why recoveries must be captured separately with a documented sign convention ([WCIRB ERP 2022](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/9-2022_erp-final.pdf); [WCIRB experience modification](https://www.wcirb.com/guide-to-workers-compensation/experience-rating/experience-modification)). Non-CA losses must be separated by jurisdiction because NCCI states keep only 30% of medical-only claims in rating and use different injury-type and class-code conventions ([NCCI ABCs](https://www.ncci.com/Articles/Documents/UW_ABC_Exp_Rating.pdf)).

## Position each vendor on what it cannot show, and let the architect present the design

The compliance argument is the build side's strongest and least obvious card, and it should be made precisely. California has not adopted the NAIC Model Bulletin (27 states plus DC had as of October 2026), but CDI Bulletin 2022-5 requires insurers to "conduct their own due diligence before using any data collection method, fraud algorithm, or rating, underwriting, or marketing tool," and Executive Order N-5-26 of March 2026 directs state agencies toward AI vendor certification standards, with whether it binds State Fund as a quasi-public entity unresolved in the sources ([NAIC adoption map](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/legal-adoption-map-ai-model-bulletin.pdf); [Debevoise on 2022-5](https://debevoise.com/insights/publications/2022/07/california-restricts-insurers); [Vinson & Elkins on N-5-26](https://velaw.com/insights/californias-new-executive-order-establishes-new-ai-vendor-certification-and-procurement-requirements)). Under the NAIC framework that most of State Fund's multi-state peers now follow, examiners may request "data lineage, provenance, and currency records," drift evaluations and, for third-party systems, due-diligence records, audit rights that the insurer must actually exercise, and monitoring of drift from vendor-side updates; "vendors cannot be used as a compliance shield" ([Lumenova](https://www.lumenova.ai/blog/naic-ai-model-bulletin-insurers-guide/); [Openlayer](https://www.openlayer.com/blog/post/naic-model-bulletin-ai-governance)). The pipeline's lineage record (page or cell, canonical field, rule outcome, reviewer action, model version) is the exam artifact, and the vendor route adds a DPA, a SOC 2 review, audit-rights negotiation, vendor-drift monitoring and renewal risk in a market where the Roots-to-Bevaya rebrand happened mid-RFP. Proportionality keeps the governance package light: loss-run extraction transcribes the applicant's own claims history and does not score or price, so it is low degree-of-potential-harm, but it must exist. Ship it as nine short artifacts: an AI-system inventory entry, a Responsible AI impact assessment on Microsoft's template, an audit and governance model naming who approves rule, format-memory and model changes, model-risk documentation with the golden-set report and failure-mode table, the lineage and audit log specification, the RBAC configuration, runbooks for new-carrier onboarding, format-change alerts and model retirement, a transparency note, and a one-paragraph third-party section whose brevity is itself the argument ([Microsoft RAI Standard v2](https://msblogs.thesourcemediaassets.com/sites/5/2022/06/Microsoft-Responsible-AI-Standard-v2-General-Requirements-3.pdf); [Azure CAF AI governance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cloud-adoption-framework/ai/platform/governance)).

Positioning against each vendor should be a question the evaluators ask, not an attack the developer makes. For Bevaya, ask for the per-field, per-line-of-business numbers behind the 93.1% benchmark, which exist only in an interactive dashboard and a robots-blocked write-up, ask whether the "99.5% accuracy" on the loss-run agent page has a methodology, ask for the single-tenant or customer-tenant option (there is none), and ask why repeat carriers cost the same credits as new ones; concede its calibrated confidence and pinned versions and match them ([Bevaya benchmarks](https://www.bevaya.ai/benchmarks); [Bevaya pricing](https://www.bevaya.ai/pricing)). For Convr, ask for the SOC 2 or ISO 27001 report, the hosting provider and the data residency, none of which is published, and ask it to re-submit the same PDF ten times and diff the JSON, which is the cheap test of its repeatability claim that the build side passes by construction ([Convr platform](https://www.convr.com/platform/)). For SortSpoke, ask how a "human oversight on every field" product meets Use Case 1's no-human quote, and note that it is the only vendor to document password-protected PDFs, which the build handles with PdfPig ([SortSpoke loss runs](https://www.sortspoke.com/solutions/loss-runs); [SortSpoke data extraction](https://www.sortspoke.com/platform/data-extraction)). For Nomad, ask for the API reference, the hosting provider and LLM subprocessors (unpublished), and any loss-run accuracy figure at all ([Nomad security](https://nomad-data.com/security)). The total-cost framing should be three-year and explicit about sensitivity: the build's cloud spend is in the **$10–45K range over three years** plus a named maintainer at 0.25–0.5 FTE and the architect's review time, against a Bevaya-style list price of **$48K–$91K per year** for one or two agents before implementation services, or enterprise deals that Pibit's own ROI model sizes at $500K per year, plus the same internal integration labor, a DPA, a security review and renewal escalation ([Bevaya pricing](https://www.bevaya.ai/pricing); [Pibit.ai](https://pibit.ai/blog/submission-intake-automation-commercial-p-and-c)). Demand page-volume-based quotes in the RFP so the comparison is apples to apples, and put the vendor-authored "$140K–$210K first-year internal build cost" figure on the slide yourself, labeled as a vendor number without methodology, because an evaluator who finds it first will trust it more ([Sensible](https://www.sensible.so/blog/the-prompt-janitor-problem-why-hybrid-document-processing-is-the-future)).

The POC scope for a one-to-two-person team is five or six carrier formats (the Berkshire Hathaway Direct, Pie and AmTrust formats already in hand plus two or three high-volume formats from the archive and one TPA or Excel variant), three policy years, 300–500 documents drawn by stratified sampling with ~20% deliberately hard items, a blind hold-out of at least 100 documents, and Use Case 2 as a thin slice rather than a full build. Eight weeks matches the practitioner timebox and the vendors' own 4–12 week go-lives, and the requirement document's "POC duration TBD" is an invitation to propose it ([Adlib](https://www.adlibsoftware.com/news/how-to-evaluate-ai-document-automation-platform); [Bevaya implementation](https://www.bevaya.ai/implementation)).

| Week | Milestone | Evidence produced |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Protocol, sampling script, ground-truth alignment and 10% double-keying signed off with the architect and UW lead; quota and abuse-monitoring requests filed; baseline rekeying minutes and quote cycle time measured | Frozen scorecard, sealed blind set, week-0 baseline |
| 2–3 | v1 extractor re-platformed onto CU plus format memory for the five or six carriers; per-document meters and latency measured | Format registry with versions, measured cost and p50/p95 per path |
| 4 | Demo 1: known-format replay, lineage click-through, exception queue | Internal checkpoint with underwriters |
| 5 | Rule catalog wired as validators, quote-ready payload, REST contract, webhook and Service Bus adapter to the sandbox | API spec, canonical mapping dictionary, sample code |
| 6 | Demo 2: unseen-format learn path, analytics slice with one what-if, override audit, RBAC | Analytics catalog, UX demo, RBAC configuration |
| 7 | Blind-set scoring with UW and architect present; load test | Latency and accuracy report, performance test results |
| 8 | Report, three-year TCO, governance pack, 30-day shadow-period proposal | Audit and governance model, sample UW report package |

The demo itself should run live for about 25 minutes on the evaluators' documents, with batch runs pre-recorded, because practitioner guides explicitly warn evaluators about cherry-picked clean samples, single blended accuracy numbers, and silent failures that return success with wrong data ([AlterSquare](https://altersquare.io/blog/document-ai-audit-template-evaluate-vendor-pipeline-edge-cases); [LlamaIndex](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/intelligent-document-processing-platform)). The sequence: an evaluator drops a known-carrier synthetic loss run and sees extraction in seconds with a "format memory hit: AmTrust v2024, 0 LLM calls" badge; clicks any field to see the page or cell highlight, the confidence and the rule that validated it; drops an unseen synthetic format and watches the learn path produce a new version with a "next replay is deterministic" badge; drops a document with a deliberately broken total and watches the rule fire, the item land in the queue, a reviewer correct it, and the correction be logged and promoted; sees the quote-ready JSON posted to a mock webhook; opens the analytics view and moves one what-if slider; and finishes on the audit log of everything just done with user, time, old-to-new value and model version. The accuracy slide is a per-field by per-carrier matrix with error types, STP and exception rates at the chosen threshold, p50/p95 latency, cost per document, the ground-truth error margin and an explicit "what we did not test" list, and it volunteers the worst carrier first. The external-hosting constraint is survivable and should be framed as the build side's "vendor sandbox," which the requirement document already anticipates and which bake-off norms treat as standard ("the vendor typically supplies its own cloud environment") ([Real Story Group](https://www.realstorygroup.com/Blog/tech-selection-tips-14-run-competitive-bake-offs)). Host it in an Azure subscription, not a non-Azure host, so the architect sees the same services the production design names; use only synthetic or de-identified documents; put it behind Entra ID or at minimum basic auth over HTTPS with IP allow-listing and no persistence of uploads; and show a one-page production-target diagram (insurer tenant, US Data Zone, private endpoints, CMK, modified abuse monitoring, RBAC) that states which controls the sandbox does not have. The single most important political move is to ask the Azure architect to co-own the target diagram and the scoring protocol before the demo and to present the production-design slide personally, which addresses the "developer, not architect of record" constraint by making the architect the sponsor rather than the judge.

## Seven failure modes to pre-empt before the first demo

The documented ways document-AI POCs and internal builds die are specific enough to design against, and the proposal should show each pre-emption as a commitment rather than wait to be asked. Insurance pilots stall because "pilots aren't products": launch budgets fund development but not monitoring, retraining, documentation, support or retirement, and "once it implements, those resources are gone" ([Insurance Business / Galbraith](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/why-insurance-ai-pilots-stall-after-deployment-585025.aspx)). The single-contractor maintainer is the exact key-person failure the literature names, and it is the one risk the developer's own situation makes acute.

| Failure mode | Evidence | Pre-emption built into the POC |
|---|---|---|
| No owner after the POC; contractor key-person risk | Internal builds decay "without an owner"; maintenance "depends on a few engineers who can leave" ([Faschi](https://www.fabiofaschi.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-in-insurance-who-built-it-who-bought-it-and-who-is-winning)) | Name a State Fund employee as product owner from week 0; repo, IaC and runbooks in the insurer's tenant from day one; propose a 12-month hypercare line and a 0.25–0.5 FTE maintenance budget; architect co-owns the design |
| Model retirement and prompt drift | 18-month GA lifecycle, 60 days' notice, auto-upgrade of Standard deployments; gpt-5.2 retires 8 Jun 2027 ([Model retirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/openai/concepts/model-retirements)) | Model names parameterized; golden-set regression on every model or prompt change; retirement calendar in the runbook; the replay path has no model dependency at all |
| Format churn underestimated | Hundreds of loss-run formats in production; "plan for format proliferation" ([Sensible](https://sensible.so/blog/how-two-insurance-companies-expedited-underwriting-with-automated-loss-run-processing)) | Versioned registry, header-signature diff and validation pass-rate alerts per carrier, delta repair with the prior spec as a prior, measured minutes-to-onboard a new carrier shown live |
| Silent failures and the format-detector trap | "Without a fallback, a format detector turns every unseen format into a silent failure" ([Width.ai](https://www.width.ai/post/what-is-intelligent-document-processing-idp)); success status with wrong data is the riskiest outcome | Validators run on every replay; fingerprint threshold at 0.95 with an embedding fallback; "wrong-but-plausible" tracked as its own error class; no document is accepted without reconciliation |
| Clean-data illusion and cherry-picked demos | Vendors demo on clean documents; 98% clean-PDF vs 82% real-intake gaps; evaluators are told to insist on their own uncleaned files ([LlamaIndex](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/intelligent-document-processing-platform)) | Evaluators pick the demo documents from the stratified pool; 20% edge cases mixed in; sampling script published; worst carrier shown first |
| Measurement theater | 42% of insurers measure nothing; logins and pilot counts substitute for cycle time ([Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/gb-en/insights/research-library/world-property-and-casualty-insurance-report/)) | Week-0 baseline of rekeying minutes per submission and quote cycle time; the same metrics reported at week 8; a 30-day shadow period against the manual process before cut-over |
| Over-promised accuracy and a vendor-run benchmark anchor | Bevaya's 93.1% is a vendor-run macro number on mixed-LOB documents with no independent verification ([Bevaya benchmarks](https://www.bevaya.ai/benchmarks)) | Commit only to blind-set, per-field, per-input-type numbers with confidence intervals and the ground-truth error margin; never quote a single blended number |

Four smaller traps are specific to this stack and cheap to avoid. The DI "Query Pages" meter at $200 per 1,000 pages can silently multiply a bill twentyfold if `queryFields` is used as a zero-shot extractor, so confirm which meter is hit before any use. The Durable Task Scheduler's 1 MB payload limit will be exceeded by a single Layout result for a 30-page scan, so documents and results pass by Blob URI only. CU's 1,000 pages-per-minute quota and gpt-5.2's 250K TPM default are the first ceilings a 25-user load test will hit, and both are week-one tickets. And `Microsoft.Agents.AI.DurableTask`, the preview package that would make the v2 chat assistant durable, targets .NET 10 only with no stable release, so the chat demo should use the stable `Microsoft.Agents.AI` core and stay off the critical path ([Agents.AI.DurableTask](https://www.nuget.org/packages/Microsoft.Agents.AI.DurableTask)). Change management deserves one commitment too: underwriting assistants should co-design the exception screen and see their corrections honored in format memory, because underwriters "work around tools that add review steps" and 72% of insurer AI spend goes to technology versus 28% to change ([Fulcrum Digital](https://fulcrumdigital.com/blogs/ai-underwriting-in-pc-insurance-the-gap-between-production-and-scale/)).

## Conclusion

The deepest implication of the research is that the bake-off's center of gravity is not extraction accuracy, where a fine-tuned vendor model will be competitive and may edge the build on scanned long-tail formats, but the three things that sit on top of extraction: who holds the ground truth, who can show lineage and calibrated confidence to an examiner, and what a repeat carrier costs in dollars, seconds and model dependency. The keyed archive makes the build side the only party that can score anyone, the lineage record is the regulatory artifact rather than a UX flourish, and the format registry turns the industry's loudest objection to internal builds, that models decay and formats drift, into the build's structural advantage, because a replayed format has no model to retire. Proposing the scoring protocol, filling in the acceptance thresholds, and handing the production design to the architect reframes the developer's role from "contractor who built a demo" to "author of the evaluation the vendors will be measured against."

The one uncomfortable truth to carry into the room is that the "minimize LLM calls" design does not win on raw cloud cost at list prices, because per-page layout meters dominate once extraction is deterministic; it wins on determinism, latency, auditability and drift insulation, and the proposal is stronger for saying so than for implying otherwise. The strongest version of the pitch is therefore not "build instead of buy" but "own the schema, rules, workflow, audit and APIs; rent commodity models behind one interface; and keep a headless vendor as a pluggable long-tail provider if the blind set ever justifies it." That is the pattern insurers already accept, it is the pattern Microsoft's own accelerators follow, and it leaves procurement with an option rather than a loss.
