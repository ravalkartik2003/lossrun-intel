# Winning an Internal Build-vs-Buy POC Bake-Off for Loss Run Ingestion (California WC insurer, Azure build vs. Nomad Data / SortSpoke / Convr / Bevaya)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Context: the insurer is State Compensation Insurance Fund (SCIF, California WC). The build side is a single remote developer (not architect of record); a senior Azure cloud architect owns architecture. A prior vendor evaluation (~March 2026) failed; the RFP is being re-run with new vendors while the internal POC competes. Note: the web search budget for this session was exhausted after ~45 searches; a handful of planned follow-up searches (public loss-run LLM accuracy benchmarks, Azure non-AI service unit costs) could not be run and are flagged in Gaps.

## KQ1. What insurers' evaluators (CIO/CTO, head of UW, enterprise architecture, security, procurement) actually weigh in build-vs-buy for AI document processing

### Takeaway
Evaluators weigh (in roughly this order of evidence) extraction accuracy on *their* documents, insurance-specific pre-training, speed to ROI/production, data security/explainability, and whether anyone will own the thing after the pilot. The strongest external evidence (MIT NANDA, BCG, Capgemini) currently favors buying, so the build side must neutralize the "internal builds decay and lack owners" narrative explicitly rather than argue only on cost.

### Cited Findings
- Roots/Bevaya survey of 240+ insurance executives (late 2024): top success factors when selecting an AI solution were **accuracy of AI (71%)**, **access to AI trained specifically for insurance (45%)**, **speed to ROI (43%)**, **speed to production (42%)**, and **ability to build and manage in-house (21%)**; only **21% of IT professionals** wanted to build in-house with public models; 45% still exploring, 25% testing, 22% in production — [Roots State of AI Adoption in Insurance 2025 (PDF)](https://www.roots.ai/hubfs/Reports%20and%20Whitepapers/Roots%20-%20State%20of%20AI%20Adoption%20in%20Insurance%202025.pdf) (vendor survey; recommendation to "buy pre-trained insurance models" is vendor advice, not a survey result)
- Same survey, barriers to adoption: limited skills/resources 52%, data challenges 40%, "technology does not deliver on promised value" 38%, regulatory 36%, internal resistance 35%, security/compliance 32%, business case 26% — [Roots 2025 report](https://www.roots.ai/hubfs/Reports%20and%20Whitepapers/Roots%20-%20State%20of%20AI%20Adoption%20in%20Insurance%202025.pdf)
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" (≈150 interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 deployments): ~95% of enterprise GenAI pilots show no measurable P&L impact; tools **bought from specialized vendors or built via partnerships succeed ~67% of the time vs. internal builds "one-third as often" (~22%)**; failure driver is enterprise integration and a "learning gap," not model quality; over half of GenAI budgets went to sales/marketing while back-office automation showed the biggest ROI — [Fortune, Aug 18 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo)
- Insurance-specific synthesis citing the same MIT data plus BCG: "only 7% of insurers have scaled AI, roughly two-thirds still piloting"; internal builds fail because "models decay as document formats drift and models are deprecated, and maintenance depends on a few engineers who can leave"; winning pattern is "buy the technology layer, keep data/judgment/validation accountability in-house"; under ASOP No. 56 validation responsibility stays with the carrier — [Fabio Faschi, State of AI in Insurance](https://www.fabiofaschi.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-in-insurance-who-built-it-who-bought-it-and-who-is-winning)
- Capgemini World P&C Insurance Report 2026: only ~10% of P&C insurers are AI "trailblazers"; most initiatives "stay stuck at the proof-of-concept stage"; 40% of leaders say AI meets expectations; **42% have not measured AI outcomes at all**; 72% of AI spend goes to technology vs 28% to change management — [Capgemini WPCIR 2026](https://www.capgemini.com/gb-en/insights/research-library/world-property-and-casualty-insurance-report/)
- Fulcrum Digital summary of Capgemini + Verisk/Reuters surveys: **data privacy and security was the top concern**, followed by accuracy, reliability and explainability; 55% of P&C insurers report no clear ROI and 55% say responsibility for AI initiatives is unclear; insurers that scale are ~3x more likely to run explainable-AI infrastructure — [Fulcrum Digital](https://fulcrumdigital.com/blogs/ai-underwriting-in-pc-insurance-the-gap-between-production-and-scale/)
- Celent 4th Annual AI in Insurance Survey, P&C (June 30 2026): "the vast majority of insurers are still in the initial phases of AI adoption"; larger insurers deep into agentic AI; mid/small mostly using Copilot/GPT assistants (detailed figures are only in an image on the page) — [Celent](https://www.celent.com/en/insights/4thannualaipc)
- Datos Insights (Nov 2023): three IDP solution classes — general-purpose (Microsoft/AWS/Google; "take significantly longer to deliver value"), insurance-vertical, fit-for-purpose ("can reach accuracy quickly but can be expensive"); advice: don't skip cost-benefit analysis, plan for delays in reaching accuracy — [Datos Insights blog](https://datos-insights.com/blog/when-it-comes-to-processing-insurance-documents-its-ai-to-the-rescue/); related report notes "adoption is growing among insurers, but **price points remain a barrier**" and names ABBYY, AWS, CogniSure, Google, Groundspeed, Hyperscience, Infrrd, IntellectAI, Kofax, mea, Microsoft, Pibit.ai, Send — [Datos IDP report](https://datos-insights.com/reports/intelligent-document-processing-ingestion-powered-by-ai/)
- Forrester (Boris Evelson, Nov 20 2025): GenAI/agentic AI is "forcing buyers to reconsider buy vs. build options" and "becoming an equalizer that challenges vendors' ability to differentiate"; "the few remaining IDP specialists" — [Forrester blog](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/ai-changes-the-intelligent-document-processing-idp-market)
- Novarica CIO checklist for third-party providers: use case with financial justification → measurable POC success criteria → procurement requirements → SLA capability → architecture robustness → data origin/rights → evaluate data → confirm POC met criteria → references; "Too much is riding on third-party data decisions for insurers to take an ad hoc approach" — [Insurance-Canada summary of Novarica](https://insurance-canada.ca/2020/07/02/novarica-third-party-data-providers-cio-checklist/)
- Duck Creek (vendor) frames build as justified only when all three hold: true differentiator configuration can't deliver, sizable stable engineering capacity for multi-year ownership, and funding for continuous upgrades indefinitely — [Duck Creek](https://www.duckcreek.com/resource/blog/build-vs-buy-in-insurance-how-leaders-decide-on-core-systems-ai-and-digital-platforms/) (vendor bias; no data)
- Underwriters spend ~40% of time on non-core/administrative tasks (Accenture/The Institutes 2021 survey of 434 P&C underwriters; commercial lines ~40%, personal ~45%) — [survey PDF](https://riskandinsurance.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/TI-Accenture_PC_Underwriter_Survey-_vf.pdf); Accenture's Corey Barker: some data ingestion tools have 90–95% success rate; AI could cut submission/quote turnaround 20–40% (estimate) — [Insurance Business](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/using-ai-for-underwriters-could-free-up-to-40-of-your-day-430784.aspx)

### Inferences
- The evaluator mental model is "buying is the safe default"; the internal POC wins only if it beats vendors on *accuracy-on-our-documents* and *credibly answers ownership/maintenance*, because those two items are exactly where the external evidence says internal builds die.
- "Insurance-specific pre-training" (45%) is a vendor advantage the build side can partially neutralize by demonstrating a carrier-independent canonical schema plus the R-01..R-15 accounting validation catalog — domain logic that is *more* insurance-specific than a generic vendor model.
- The head of UW cares about the 40%-admin-time number and quote-cycle time; CIO/architecture cares about integration and ownership; security cares about data location and DPA; procurement cares about exit rights and price predictability. The proposal should have one page addressed to each.
- The 21%-of-IT-wants-to-build figure means the internal developer should not pitch "build everything"; pitch "own the data/validation/workflow layer, rent commodity models."

- No published, insurer-specific weighted scorecard from an analyst firm was found; the weights in KQ5 come from IDP practitioner/vendor sources.

## KQ2. Typical vendor pricing and implementation timelines for loss run / submission intake products

### Takeaway
Almost no intake vendor publishes prices; the one public data point (Bevaya) is **$4,000/agent/month billed annually with 100k–200k pooled credits (1 credit ≈ 1 standard page)**, with multi-agent and volume discounts. Implementation claims cluster at **4–12 weeks** (Bevaya 4–12, Convr 4–6 weeks to first workflow / 90 days to full, SortSpoke "live in 30 minutes" for its SaaS), and every vendor's accuracy number (95–99.5%) is unaudited.

### Inferences
- Working TCO comparator for the buy side (assumptions explicit): using Bevaya's public list price, one loss-run agent ≈ **$48,000/yr** at ≤100k–200k pages; two agents (UC1 quoting + UC2 analytics) ≈ **$91,200/yr** with the 5% two-agent discount; plus unlisted implementation services and the insurer's own integration labor. Nomad/Convr/SortSpoke are quote-only; the Pibit ROI model's "$500K/yr software + services" and the "PAS add-on $500K–$1.5M" are the only published magnitude anchors for enterprise carrier deals, and are vendor-sourced.
- At ~50,000 archived documents over two years (≈25,000/yr), and assuming 10–20 pages per loss run, annual page volume is ~250k–500k pages, which would exceed Bevaya's included credits and push into the Growth tier — the build side should demand page-volume-based vendor quotes in the RFP so apples-to-apples comparison is possible.
- Vendor "4–12 week" go-lives assume the carrier supplies samples, SMEs and system access — the same inputs the internal POC needs; the build side should point out that the vendor timeline does not include procurement, security review, DPA negotiation, and integration, which for a California state entity can add months (see KQ4 on EO N-5-26).

## KQ3. TCO framing for the build side: Azure consumption at 1k / 10k / 50k docs per year, engineering & maintenance, 3-year TCO, where build wins or loses

### Takeaway
Azure consumption for loss-run extraction is small — on the order of **$0.02–$0.10 per document** at list prices for Document Intelligence Layout plus a GPT-4.1-mini pass, so even 50,000 docs/yr costs low-single-digit thousands; the 3-year TCO is dominated by people (a maintainer at 0.25–0.5 FTE) and by model-retirement migrations, not by cloud spend. Build wins on variable cost and data control; it loses if there is no named owner, no drift monitoring, and no plan for the 18-month model lifecycle.

### Inferences (worked consumption estimate — all assumptions stated)
Assumptions: average loss run = 12 pages (range 5–50+); first-seen carrier format gets Layout + a full LLM extraction pass; a *known* format (per-carrier "format memory") gets Layout + a cheap validation-only LLM call or none; tokens per page ≈ 1,000 input, ≈ 300 output for extraction; GPT-4.1 mini Data Zone (US) prices; Document Intelligence Layout $10/1,000 pages.
- Per *new-format* document (12 pp): Layout $0.12 + LLM (12k in × $0.44/M = $0.0053; 3.6k out × $1.76/M = $0.0063) ≈ **$0.13**.
- Per *known-format* document with LLM validation pass on a condensed table (≈4k in / 0.5k out): Layout $0.12 + ≈$0.003 ≈ **$0.12** — i.e., **Document Intelligence, not the LLM, dominates per-document cost**; the "minimize LLM calls" design mainly buys latency, determinism and auditability, not dollars. If Read ($1.50/1,000) suffices for born-digital PDFs and Excel is parsed natively, the per-doc floor drops to ≈ $0.02–$0.03.
- Annual consumption (Layout path, 12 pp avg): **1,000 docs ≈ $130; 10,000 docs ≈ $1,300; 50,000 docs ≈ $6,500**; add ~30% for retries/re-extraction/evaluation runs and ~$150–$400/month for always-on platform plumbing (Container Apps/Functions, storage, queue, Cosmos/SQL, Key Vault, App Insights — order-of-magnitude, not sourced; see Gaps). Even the 50k case lands roughly **$10k–$15k/yr** in cloud spend.
- Using GPT-4.1 (full) instead of mini multiplies LLM cost ~5x but LLM is still <10% of per-doc cost on the Layout path.
- 3-year TCO framing recommended: **Build** = cloud ($10–45k over 3 yrs) + build effort (one developer, already sunk for v1/v2) + maintenance at 0.25–0.5 FTE (format churn, model migrations every ~18 months, rule catalog updates) + the architect's review time; **Buy** = Bevaya-style list ($48k–$91k/yr → $144k–$274k over 3 yrs) or enterprise quotes in the Pibit "$500k/yr" range + implementation services + the same internal integration/UAT labor + DPA/security review + renewal escalation risk. Present both with explicit sensitivity on (a) page volume, (b) maintainer FTE, (c) vendor renewal uplift.
- Where build loses: if the maintainer is a single contractor (key-person risk is the exact failure mode cited by Faschi and Galbraith) — mitigate by naming a State Fund-employed owner, documenting runbooks, and proposing a 12-month hypercare budget line. Where build wins: variable cost scales with volume, no per-page vendor margin, no DPA, format memory makes marginal cost of a repeat carrier near zero, and the R-01..R-15 rule catalog is reusable IP the insurer keeps.

- Microsoft's official Document Intelligence pricing page rendered as placeholders; third-party figures should be verified in the Azure Pricing Calculator for West US/East US before presenting.
- No sourced unit costs were retrieved for the non-AI plumbing (Container Apps, Functions, Cosmos DB, Blob, Service Bus); the search budget ran out. The platform-plumbing estimate above is an unsourced order-of-magnitude placeholder.
- No independent 3-year TCO study for IDP build vs buy exists in the sources found; the Sensible "$140–210k first-year build" figure is a vendor number without methodology.

## KQ4. Risk/compliance arguments: data in-tenant, no third-party DPA, inherited controls, PII/PHI, vendor lock-in/viability, regulatory explainability (NAIC bulletin, California, Colorado) and how a lineage-bearing internal pipeline addresses them

### Takeaway
The build side's strongest compliance story is that Azure OpenAI/Foundry data stays in the insurer's tenant and US data zone, is not used for training, is covered by the existing Microsoft DPA, and that the NAIC Model Bulletin makes third-party AI *more* paperwork (due diligence, audit rights, drift monitoring) rather than less; a transparent pipeline with field-level lineage directly produces the "data lineage, provenance and currency records" and "model drift evaluation" regulators may request. California has *not* adopted the NAIC bulletin but has its own Bulletin 2022-5 and a March 2026 executive order creating AI vendor certification standards for state procurement.

### Cited Findings
**Azure data handling**
- Azure OpenAI / Foundry official data-privacy doc: prompts, completions, embeddings and training data "are NOT used by providers of Models sold by Azure to improve their models," "are NOT available to other customers," "are NOT available to OpenAI"; models are stateless; Standard deployments process within customer-specified geography; **DataZone** processes anywhere within the zone (e.g., "anywhere in the United States") with data at rest in the designated geography; abuse monitoring uses automated review first with human review only on flagged content, and customers can be approved for **modified abuse monitoring** (no storage, no human review) verifiable via `"ContentLogging": "false"` in the resource JSON; AES-256 at rest with optional customer-managed keys; governed by the Microsoft Products and Services DPA and Product Terms — [Microsoft Learn: Data, privacy, and security for Azure Direct Models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)

## KQ5. POC scoping and sequencing: minimum credible scope, blind test protocol, milestones, timeline for a 1–2 person team, and pitching a hybrid fallback

### Takeaway
Practitioner guidance converges on a **4–8 week timebox** (2 weeks setup, 2–4 testing, 1–2 scoring), a **200–500 document stratified test set with ~20% deliberate edge cases**, ground truth and metric definitions frozen *before* testing, identical samples and scoring for every contender, field-level reporting, pre-agreed go/no-go thresholds, and a weighted scorecard — which the internal POC can propose to the architect and procurement as the common protocol, positioning itself as a contender under the same rules as the vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Adlib/AlterSquare PoC protocol: 200–500 representative docs per use case stratified by type and quality; pre-process identically and strip source-revealing metadata; define ground truth and metrics before testing; set go/no-go thresholds (minimum F1 per class, max exception rate, max processing time, required controls); **timebox 4–8 weeks (≈2 wks setup, 2–4 testing, 1–2 scoring)**; assign a technical evaluator, a business validation owner, and a compliance lead; require vendors to run on your data as-is with minimal configuration help — [Adlib](https://www.adlibsoftware.com/news/how-to-evaluate-ai-document-automation-platform)
- Suggested scorecard weights (Adlib): extraction accuracy (F1) 20%, validation & accuracy governance 20%, HITL & audit trail 15%, security & compliance 15%, integration depth 15%, performance & scalability 10%, cost transparency 5% — agree weights before testing — [Adlib](https://www.adlibsoftware.com/news/how-to-evaluate-ai-document-automation-platform)
- Alternative weights (AlterSquare 100-point card): accuracy 30, security/privacy 20, integration/API 15, deployment/performance 15, support/onboarding 10, commercial 10; proceed only ≥75/100; any sub-3/5 on accuracy or security is automatic no-go; tie within 5 points → data residency, exit rights, model version pinning; **run a shadow period before final approval**; test set 500–1,000 docs, ~80% standard / 20% edge cases *mixed into normal batches* so contenders can't tell which are stress tests; edge-case categories: image quality (skew, low-contrast fax), layout (tables spanning pages, multi-column), content (handwriting, stamps, margin notes), logic (conflicting totals), intentionally flawed (missing pages) — [AlterSquare](https://altersquare.io/blog/document-ai-audit-template-evaluate-vendor-pipeline-edge-cases)
- LandingAI: "a few hundred documents gives stable per-field numbers"; build answer key with every field per document; define null handling in advance (absent vs missed); normalization rules for dates/currency/whitespace; track error types separately — missing, wrong, **wrong-but-plausible ("most dangerous")**; score tables with TEDS or cell-level match; record review minutes per document; identical sample and scoring rules for every vendor — [LandingAI](https://landing.ai/geo-content-strategy/how-to-test-document-extraction-accuracy-before-choosing-a-vendor)
- KDAN: stratified sample across types/layouts/quality; ground truth by second reviewer or consensus with recorded error margin; segment by field, document type and confidence band; re-audit quarterly or after any model/template change; a 2024 study found reference sets only 86–95% accurate distort measured accuracy and human reviewers agreed only 71–86% of the time — [KDAN](https://www.kdan.com/blog/measure-audit-intelligent-document-processing-accuracy)
- LlamaIndex: test on 50–100 of your own documents minimum; vendor benchmarks don't predict performance on your corpus; calculate STP at different confidence thresholds — [LlamaIndex](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/intelligent-document-processing-platform)
- Bake-off mechanics (Real Story Group): identify and flesh out key scenarios in advance; ask finalists to mimic a real implementation sprint with hands-on work; use your own content, data and real users; run 1–2 weeks (longer for mission-critical); daily check-ins; may need to reimburse vendors at a reduced rate; address concerns about a vendor explicitly since "they tend to worsen over time" — [Real Story Group](https://www.realstorygroup.com/Blog/tech-selection-tips-14-run-competitive-bake-offs)
- InformationWeek: define business case and measurable metrics before starting and share them with vendors and staff up front; several days to two weeks; dedicated full-time bake-off team; IT and end users jointly select against predefined metrics; typically two finalists after RFP narrowing — [InformationWeek](https://www.informationweek.com/software-services/how-to-make-vendor-technology-bakeoffs-work)
- Novarica checklist step 2 and 8: "set measurable success criteria for a proof of concept" and "confirm the proof of concept met its success criteria" — [Novarica via Insurance-Canada](https://insurance-canada.ca/2020/07/02/novarica-third-party-data-providers-cio-checklist/)
- Roots recommends 1–2 week "Proof of Value" exercises (vendor advice) — [Roots 2025 report](https://www.roots.ai/hubfs/Reports%20and%20Whitepapers/Roots%20-%20State%20of%20AI%20Adoption%20in%20Insurance%202025.pdf)
- SortSpoke 30-day baseline playbook: sample 50 submissions for response-time distribution, 100 declines, shadow 3–5 underwriters one day each; compute cost of inefficiency = share × weekly volume × hours × 52, FTE = hours/2,080; one-page baseline report — [SortSpoke](https://sortspoke.com/blog/first-30-days-intake-improvement) (vendor; figures unsourced)
- Hybrid patterns in the literature: insurance "assemble" approach — build differentiating components (UW desktop, rules services), buy horizontal ones — [Digital Insurance, 2010](https://www.dig-in.com/news/buy-or-build-or-both); GeekyAnts three-layer model — own knowledge layer and workflow/guardrail layer, rent/swap the model layer — [GeekyAnts](https://geekyants.com/en-us/blog/build-vs-buy-choosing-the-right-ai-strategy-for-insurance-companies); Indico "headless" deployment into a carrier's own validation UI shows vendors will sell extraction-only behind an insurer-owned workflow — [Indico](https://indicodata.ai/customer-story-global-specialty-headless-implementation/); LlamaIndex routing architecture: format detector + specialist extractors with an LLM fallback for "the long tail of documents the specialist has not seen"; without a fallback "a format detector turns every unseen format into a silent failure" — [Width.ai](https://www.width.ai/post/what-is-intelligent-document-processing-idp)
- Sensible evidence that loss-run format counts run into the hundreds (one platform ~300 formats, started with ~50 templates) and that deterministic extraction gave better precision on dense loss runs while ACORDs suited a hybrid — [Sensible](https://sensible.so/blog/how-two-insurance-companies-expedited-underwriting-with-automated-loss-run-processing)
- Datos Insights: "plan for possible delays in reaching accuracy" — [Datos](https://datos-insights.com/blog/when-it-comes-to-processing-insurance-documents-its-ai-to-the-rescue/)
- WCIRB experience rating uses per-claim incurred (medical + indemnity) at last valuation, primary/excess split at the Primary Threshold, injury type codes (medical-only = code 6), payroll by class — i.e., the canonical fields a California WC underwriter actually consumes — [WCIRB experience rating worksheet guide](https://wcirb.com/guide-to-workers-compensation/experience-rating/experience-rating-wksheet)

### Inferences (proposed scope for a 1–2 person team)
- **Scope**: 5–6 carrier formats (the user's memory already lists Berkshire Hathaway Direct, Pie, AmTrust; add 2–3 high-volume formats from the archive and one TPA/Excel variant), 3 policy years, **300–500 documents** drawn from the 50k archive by stratified sampling (format × year × page-count × scan quality), with ~20% deliberately hard items mixed in; hold out a **blind set** (≥100 docs) that the developer never sees until scoring day and that the *same* set is given to each vendor under identical rules. Ground truth = the historical user-keyed form values already in the archive (the user's existing plan), double-checked on a 10% sub-sample by a UW assistant because keyed data is itself 86–95% accurate.
- **Metrics to freeze in week 0**: per-field exact match after normalization for the ~10 required fields; per-claim row recall; policy-total reconciliation pass rate (the R-01..R-15 rule catalog gives a built-in "did the totals tie" metric no vendor will have); STP rate at the chosen confidence threshold; exception rate (healthy pipelines run ~5–10%); p50/p95 latency per document; review minutes per exception; cost per document.
- **Sequencing (6–8 weeks)**: Wk 0–1 protocol + sample + ground truth sign-off with the architect and UW lead; Wk 2–3 run v1 extractor on training split, build format memory for the 5–6 carriers; Wk 4 milestone demo #1 (extraction + lineage + exceptions on known formats); Wk 5 integrate validation engine + quote-ready payload + webhook/REST contract; Wk 6 milestone demo #2 (unseen-format fallback path, analytics, audit log); Wk 7 blind-set scoring with UW + architect present; Wk 8 report + TCO + governance pack. Keep UC2 (analytics in the workbench) as a thin slice (3–4 canned views + one what-if) rather than a full build.
- **Hybrid pitch**: "Insurer-owned canonical schema, validation rules, exception workflow, audit log and APIs; pluggable extraction providers behind one interface — Azure Document Intelligence + LLM by default, a vendor (headless API) for long-tail or scanned formats if blind-set results justify it." This matches the pattern insurers already accept (Indico headless) and keeps the contractual option open for procurement without conceding the platform.

### Gaps
- No source gives an insurer-specific minimum number of carrier formats or policy years for a loss-run POC; the 5–6 formats / 300–500 docs figures are inferred from general IDP test-set guidance.
- No published example of an internal team being scored side-by-side with vendors under one protocol was found; the bake-off sources only consider vendor finalists.

## KQ6. Demo design that wins: live vs pre-recorded, making "no labelled data / learn once, replay" a selling point, the external-hosting constraint, and presenting accuracy honestly

### Takeaway
Evaluators are explicitly warned (by the same practitioner guides they read) against cherry-picked documents, blended accuracy figures and silent failures — so the winning demo runs the *evaluators'* documents live, shows field-level confidence and lineage, deliberately exercises an exception and an unseen format, and reports per-field/per-carrier numbers with the ground-truth error margin. The external-hosting constraint is survivable if the demo uses only synthetic/de-identified documents and is framed as the vendor-sandbox equivalent the requirement document itself anticipates.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor-demo pitfalls evaluators are told to watch for: cherry-picked clean samples ("insist on your own uncleaned files"); stress tests the vendor knows about; a single blended accuracy number hiding weak document types; character-level metrics masking field errors; **silent failures** (success status with wrong data) are riskier than flagged uncertainty; average latency hides p95 under burst; evasiveness about limitations is disqualifying — [AlterSquare](https://altersquare.io/blog/document-ai-audit-template-evaluate-vendor-pipeline-edge-cases)
- "Vendors demo on clean documents. Production intake includes poor scans, phone photos, and faxes"; illustrative 98% clean-PDF vs 82% real-intake gap; legacy systems "send exceptions to a manual queue with no confidence signal" — propose field-level confidence so reviewers focus on specific fields — [LlamaIndex](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/intelligent-document-processing-platform)
- Optivalue: "A successful POC demonstrates that the technology works. Not that it creates value in your context"; illustrative 94% demo → 61% real-conditions drop; define the POC question in operational terms (time saved, errors avoided, decision quality) and test on your own documents, users and conditions — [Optivalue](https://www.optivalue.ai/en/blog/why-ai-pocs-fail/)
- Coverager/Ushur: choosing a solution "based on impressive demonstrations" leads to disappointment; progress walk → jog → run with metrics set up front — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/insurance-automation-proof-of-concept/)
- Faschi: "deployment at the point of decision, not summaries"; "measurement at the workflow level, not adoption counts"; explainability — [Faschi](https://www.fabiofaschi.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-in-insurance-who-built-it-who-bought-it-and-who-is-winning); Insurance Thought Leadership: "Explainability, not speed, proved essential" — [ITL](https://www.insurancethoughtleadership.com/ai-machine-learning/2026-year-ai-goes-operational-insurance)
- Explainability expectations: every automated recommendation should carry "the reasoning and evidence that underwriters, regulators, and brokers need"; higher-impact decisions keep human review and an override path — [Fulcrum Digital](https://fulcrumdigital.com/blogs/ai-underwriting-in-pc-insurance-the-gap-between-production-and-scale/)
- Confidence scores must be *calibrated*: "low-confidence fields should contain more errors… check that this holds rather than assuming it" — [KDAN](https://www.kdan.com/blog/measure-audit-intelligent-document-processing-accuracy)
- Convr's own demo surface (what evaluators will see from a vendor): confidence scoring, side-by-side document vs extracted data, inline correction that feeds model improvement, exception queues, data lineage, reviewer-action logs — [Convr](https://convr.com/intake); Bevaya's: a "merged_policy_table" across policy periods and a "quote-ready loss history view" — [Bevaya](https://www.bevaya.ai/platform/ai-agents/loss-run-processing)
- Format-memory legitimacy: deterministic per-carrier templates gave better precision than LLM-only on dense loss runs at scale (~300 formats) — [Sensible](https://sensible.so/blog/how-two-insurance-companies-expedited-underwriting-with-automated-loss-run-processing); a format detector + specialist + LLM fallback architecture, where the LLM covers "the long tail… the specialist has not seen" — [Width.ai](https://www.width.ai/post/what-is-intelligent-document-processing-idp); Azure WAF: "test inference endpoints with unused PTUs or simulated endpoints to avoid per-call charges" and monitor utilization — [Azure WAF](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/ai/design-principles)
- Azure WAF security: "remove unneeded personal and confidential information from all stores, including search indexes and caches"; "require authentication for all inferencing endpoints, including system-to-system calls" — [Azure WAF](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/ai/design-principles)
- Vendor sandboxes are the norm: Real Story Group notes "the vendor typically supplies its own cloud environment" for bake-offs — [RSG](https://www.realstorygroup.com/Blog/tech-selection-tips-14-run-competitive-bake-offs); the client's own requirement doc lists "vendor sandbox + secure carrier enclave read-only" as the POC environments (from task context)
- Microsoft RAI Standard T1/T2: decision makers must be able to interpret system behaviour "without over-relying on outputs"; publish capabilities, intended uses, limitations, operating settings and "evidence of accuracy and generalizability" — [Microsoft RAI Standard v2](https://msblogs.thesourcemediaassets.com/sites/5/2022/06/Microsoft-Responsible-AI-Standard-v2-General-Requirements-3.pdf)

### Inferences (demo script)
- **Live (≈25 min)**: (1) evaluator drops a *known-carrier* synthetic loss run → extraction in seconds with "format memory hit: AmTrust v2024, 0 LLM calls" badge; (2) click any canonical field → page/cell highlight + confidence + rule that validated it (lineage); (3) an *unseen* synthetic format → fallback path with LLM, "format learned, next replay is deterministic" badge; (4) a document with a deliberately broken total → R-0x rule fires, lands in exception queue, reviewer corrects, correction is logged and (optionally) promoted into format memory; (5) "quote-ready payload" JSON posted to a mock webhook / GraphQL query; (6) UW analytics view with one what-if (e.g., exclude a closed claim, recompute 3-year incurred); (7) audit log of everything just done, with who/what/when/model-version. 
- **Pre-recorded (≈5 min, optional)**: bulk run over the 300–500 doc sample with the dashboard filling in, and the blind-set scoring run — because batch jobs are boring live and the numbers must be reproducible.
- **Accuracy slide**: per-field × per-carrier matrix (not one number), error type split (missing / wrong / wrong-but-plausible), STP and exception rates at the chosen threshold, p50/p95 latency, cost per doc, ground-truth error margin, and an explicit "what we did not test" list. Volunteer the worst carrier first.
- **"No labelled data" reframing**: the pipeline needs no training set because (a) the canonical schema + rule catalog provide *semantic* validation (totals must tie, dates must order, statuses must be in domain), and (b) format memory turns each carrier's first document into a reusable deterministic extractor that is auditable and version-pinned — the "learn once, replay" story is also the cost and drift story (zero marginal LLM calls on repeat formats; a model retirement only affects the long-tail path).
- **External hosting**: present it as the build-side "vendor sandbox" running on Azure (ideally an Azure subscription, not a non-Azure host, so the architect sees the same services the production design proposes); only synthetic/de-identified documents; put the app behind Entra ID or at least basic auth over HTTPS with IP allow-listing and no persistence of uploads; show the one-page "production target" diagram (insurer tenant, US Data Zone, private endpoints, CMK, modified abuse monitoring, RBAC) so security sees the sandbox is a demo and not the architecture. Say explicitly which controls are not in the sandbox.
- **Architect alignment**: ask the architect to co-own the target architecture diagram and the scoring protocol before the demo; let the architect present the production design slide — this converts the owner of architecture decisions from a judge into a sponsor, and addresses the "developer, not architect of record" constraint.

### Gaps
- No source specifically evaluates demoing from an external host versus client network for security optics; the guidance above is inferred from WAF/CAF controls and bake-off norms.
- No independent evidence that "format memory" beats pure-LLM on accuracy for loss runs beyond Sensible's (vendor) claim.

## KQ7. Common failure modes of document-AI POCs and internal builds, and how to pre-empt them

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are: demo ≠ value, clean-data illusion, no exception/HITL design, no owner or budget after go-live, model/format drift with no monitoring, measurement theater, and change-management neglect; each has a concrete pre-emption the proposal can commit to (named owner, hypercare budget line, drift tests, per-field reporting, shadow period).

### Cited Findings
- Structural POC failures: technical demo mistaken for proof of value; sponsor backs the idea but not the change; industrialisation rushed or never planned ("decide who runs the system, how anomalies are detected, who validates outputs, who handles model drift"); real data differs from test data; use case not anchored in real document pain — [Optivalue](https://www.optivalue.ai/en/blog/why-ai-pocs-fail/)
- Insurance pilots stall because "pilots aren't products" (need account owner, support, update process, performance-issue path); project team disappears; launch budgets fund build but not monitoring/retraining/documentation/support/retirement; AI changes over time — [Insurance Business / Galbraith](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/why-insurance-ai-pilots-stall-after-deployment-585025.aspx)
- Faschi failure list: pilot purgatory; hero projects (chatbots while intake teams rekey forms); "dying internal builds: strong demos decay in production without an owner"; measurement theater (logins and pilot counts instead of cycle time); governance last (Earnix: 92% run governance reviews but <1 in 3 believe they keep pace); shadow AI; learning gap — [Faschi](https://www.fabiofaschi.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-in-insurance-who-built-it-who-bought-it-and-who-is-winning)
- Internal-build specific: formats drift and models get deprecated; maintenance rests on few engineers; production AI is "ongoing LLM operations" — [Faschi](https://www.fabiofaschi.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-in-insurance-who-built-it-who-bought-it-and-who-is-winning); "prompt janitor" upkeep: prompts, model retirements, schema normalization, derived-value post-processing; treat document processing "as infrastructure, with versioning, monitoring, SLAs, and testing" — [Sensible](https://www.sensible.so/blog/the-prompt-janitor-problem-why-hybrid-document-processing-is-the-future)
- Azure model lifecycle facts that make drift concrete: 18-month GA lifecycle, 60-day retirement notice, auto-upgrade of Standard deployments to a newer model at retirement — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/openai/concepts/model-retirements)
- Scaling failures in UW AI: poor submission data erodes cycle-time gains; underwriters "work around tools that add review steps"; 72/28 tech-vs-change-management spend; unclear ownership and no baseline — [Fulcrum Digital](https://fulcrumdigital.com/blogs/ai-underwriting-in-pc-insurance-the-gap-between-production-and-scale/); 42% of insurers measure nothing — [Capgemini](https://www.capgemini.com/gb-en/insights/research-library/world-property-and-casualty-insurance-report/)
- MIT NANDA: integration and workflow adaptation, not model quality, decide outcomes; tools that "integrate deeply and adapt over time" win — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo)
- Silent failures, blended averages, average-latency masking, null-handling false positives — [AlterSquare](https://altersquare.io/blog/document-ai-audit-template-evaluate-vendor-pipeline-edge-cases); wrong-but-plausible errors are the dangerous class — [LandingAI](https://landing.ai/geo-content-strategy/how-to-test-document-extraction-accuracy-before-choosing-a-vendor)
- Demo-chosen tools disappoint; skipping POC and forcing tools into old roles/workflows — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/insurance-automation-proof-of-concept/); "proof of concept instead of proof of value" (2015 but still cited) — [ITL / Polyakov](https://www.insurancethoughtleadership.com/going-digital/how-avoid-pitfalls-insurance-innovation)
- Datos: fit-for-purpose tools may lack pre-trained models and need "substantial training time before reaching potentially higher accuracy" — plan for delays — [Datos](https://datos-insights.com/blog/when-it-comes-to-processing-insurance-documents-its-ai-to-the-rescue/)
- Format proliferation: hundreds of loss-run formats; "plan for format proliferation… design for edge cases early" (e.g., claim-free policies returning policy metadata not null) — [Sensible](https://sensible.so/blog/how-two-insurance-companies-expedited-underwriting-with-automated-loss-run-processing)
- NARA pilot guidance (archived but structurally useful): tie objectives to metrics, baseline → interim → final evaluation, final report on functional/technical/management expectations, lessons-learned appendix grouped by users, implementation, technology — [NARA](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/policy/pilot-guidance.html)

### Inferences (pre-emption table for the proposal)
- *Over-promised accuracy* → commit only to blind-set, per-field numbers with error margins; include a "worst carrier" slide.
- *Cherry-picked docs* → evaluators pick the demo documents from the stratified pool; 20% edge cases mixed in; publish the sampling script.
- *Exception handling ignored* → exception queue, reviewer UI, correction-to-memory loop and SLA are first-class deliverables (the requirement doc already lists "exception workflow").
- *Latency ignored* → report p50/p95 per document and per page, and a synthetic real-time submission simulator run (the requirement doc lists this environment).
- *Format churn underestimated* → show the format-memory registry with versioning, a "format changed" detector (layout hash mismatch → fallback path + alert) and the cost of onboarding a new carrier in minutes, measured.
- *Model drift / retirement* → version-pin models, golden-set regression run on every model or prompt change, retirement-calendar check in the runbook, evaluate replacement on own data per Microsoft guidance.
- *No owner after POC* → name a State Fund employee as product owner, propose a 12-month hypercare line and a 0.25–0.5 FTE maintenance budget, document runbooks; propose a 30-day shadow period against the current manual process before cut-over.
- *Measurement theater* → baseline rekeying minutes per submission and quote cycle time in week 0 (SortSpoke's 50-submission / 3–5 underwriter shadowing method is cheap), and report the same metrics at the end.
- *Change management* → UW assistants co-design the exception screen; show their corrections being honored.
- *Key-person risk (contractor)* → repo, IaC, and docs in the insurer's tenant from day one; architect co-owns design; pair with an employee.

## KQ8. Governance artefacts evaluators expect: audit & governance model, model risk documentation, RBAC config, runbooks — templates/examples

### Takeaway
A credible governance pack can be assembled from four public frameworks: NAIC Bulletin Section 4 (what examiners ask for), Colorado 10-1-1 (the most prescriptive artefact list: inventory, testing description, vendor-selection process, annual review), Microsoft's Responsible AI Standard v2 + Impact Assessment template (A1–A5, T1–T3, RS1–RS3 map cleanly onto human oversight, explainability, failure/remediation and monitoring), and Azure CAF/WAF (RBAC, PIM, managed identities, private endpoints, model lifecycle, drift detection, ISO/IEC 42001).

### Cited Findings
- NAIC Section 4 request examples: written AIS Program + evidence of adoption; adverse-decision explanation template; data lineage/provenance/currency records; model-level compliance docs, bias analysis, drift evaluation; third-party due diligence records, contracts, audit documentation — [Lumenova](https://www.lumenova.ai/blog/naic-ai-model-bulletin-insurers-guide/); examiners may also request AI inventory with named owners, pre-deployment testing results and thresholds, monitoring logs and drift alerts, vendor accountability records, consumer complaint logs — [Openlayer](https://www.openlayer.com/blog/post/naic-model-bulletin-ai-governance); AIS Program must include inventories and documentation of predictive models, validation and testing, protection of non-public information — [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/NAIC-Model-Bulletin-Use-AI-Insurers.pdf)
- Colorado 10-1-1 artefact list (useful as a template even though WC is out of scope): board oversight; senior-management accountability; cross-functional governance group with legal/compliance; written lifecycle policies with assigned roles; complaint/inquiry process; **inventory with descriptions, purposes, outputs, material changes, version control**; testing methodology/assumptions/results/remediation; ongoing drift monitoring; documented third-party vendor selection process; documented annual review — [Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/9/colorado-division-of-insurance-expands-ai-related-governance-and-risk-management-obligations-for-insurers)
- Microsoft Responsible AI Standard v2 requirements: **A1** impact assessment reviewed annually; **A3** fit for purpose with release criteria; **A4** data governance; **A5** human oversight — identify who troubleshoots/operates/oversees, design for override/interrupt and automation-bias awareness, evaluate oversight under realistic conditions; **T1** intelligibility for decision makers and affected people; **T2** publish capabilities, intended uses, limitations, operating settings, evidence of accuracy/generalizability, reviewed annually (Transparency Note); **RS1** define operating ranges and acceptable error rates; **RS2** enumerate predictable failures incl. false positives/negatives, design fallbacks with documented invocation time, plan rollback/feature shutoff/model updates/customer notification; **RS3** monitoring inventory, event review/escalation procedures, decide support for new uses; **PS1/PS2** privacy and security policy compliance — [Microsoft RAI Standard v2 PDF](https://msblogs.thesourcemediaassets.com/sites/5/2022/06/Microsoft-Responsible-AI-Standard-v2-General-Requirements-3.pdf)
- Microsoft Responsible AI Impact Assessment template (17 pages, 5 sections): system information & lifecycle; intended uses with stakeholder map, fitness-for-purpose, RAI goal mapping and deployment-complexity ratings (task complexity, human oversight model, tech readiness); adverse impact (restricted/unsupported uses, limitations, failure modes esp. false positives/negatives, misuse); data requirements; summary of impact with harm-mitigation table, applicability matrix, multi-role sign-off, annual update prompts; PDF at https://www.aigl.blog/content/files/2025/06/Microsoft-Responsible-AI-Impact-Assessment-Template.pdf — [AI Governance Library](https://www.aigl.blog/microsoft-responsible-ai-impact-assessment-template/)
- Azure CAF AI governance artefacts: AI inventory (Entra Agent ID), Azure Policy allow-lists for approved models/publishers, guardrail policies, Defender for Cloud AI posture, least-privilege RBAC with custom roles, managed identities, PIM approval workflows for production model changes, private endpoints, model lifecycle/rollback procedures, DR and alert rules, Purview Compliance Manager, ISO/IEC 42001 audit; explicit gaps to fill yourself: RAI standard, model risk management (validation, bias testing, model cards), insurance-specific mapping, AI incident response, third-party AI risk, data retention/residency/PIA — [Azure CAF](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cloud-adoption-framework/ai/platform/governance)
- Azure WAF operational excellence: define quality metrics early; experiment tracking (code versions, environments, parameters, runs, results); automated drift tests with alerts; safe side-by-side deployments; emergency operations plan; collect only compliant interaction data — [Azure WAF](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/ai/design-principles)
- Azure OpenAI controls to document: Data Zone deployment, modified abuse monitoring approval (verify `ContentLogging=false`), CMK, DPA coverage — [Microsoft data-privacy doc](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)
- Audit-trail expectation for every test: record input data, protected-class breakdown (where relevant), metrics, thresholds, reviewer who cleared/escalated; "a test never written to an audit trail carries no weight in an examination" — [Openlayer](https://www.openlayer.com/blog/post/naic-model-bulletin-ai-governance) (vendor)
- Audit cadence: re-audit quarterly or after any model/template/source change; segment by field, type, confidence band — [KDAN](https://www.kdan.com/blog/measure-audit-intelligent-document-processing-accuracy)
- Procurement-side expectations that the build side can mirror for itself: SOC 2 Type II/ISO 27001 (inherited from Azure + insurer), pen-test summary, data ownership, no-training clause, exit/export rights, model version pinning — [Adlib](https://www.adlibsoftware.com/news/how-to-evaluate-ai-document-automation-platform), [AlterSquare](https://altersquare.io/blog/document-ai-audit-template-evaluate-vendor-pipeline-edge-cases)

### Inferences (artefact list to ship with the POC deliverables)
1. **AI system inventory entry** (Colorado-style fields: purpose, inputs/outputs, models + versions, owner, material changes, version control) — one page.
2. **Responsible AI impact assessment** using Microsoft's template, scoped to "low degree of potential harm; human-in-the-loop; no pricing/eligibility decisions."
3. **Audit & governance model**: who approves rule-catalog changes (R-01..R-15), format-memory promotions, prompt/model version changes; change log; quarterly accuracy re-audit; retirement calendar check.
4. **Model risk documentation**: golden-set evaluation report (per field × carrier), confidence-calibration check, drift tests, failure-mode table (RS2) with fallbacks (vendor/LLM path, manual queue) and rollback times.
5. **Lineage & audit log spec**: document → page/cell → canonical field → rule outcomes → reviewer action → payload version, retained per records policy (maps to NAIC 1.3b).
6. **RBAC configuration**: Entra groups for UW assistant (review/correct), underwriter (read + what-if), admin (format memory/rules), service identities (managed identity only), PIM for production changes; private endpoints; CMK; Data Zone; modified abuse monitoring.
7. **Runbooks**: new-carrier onboarding; format-change alert handling; model retirement migration (60-day notice → evaluate on golden set → switch → rollback); exception-queue SLA breach; DR.
8. **Transparency note** (T2): capabilities, limitations, unsupported document types, evidence of accuracy.
9. **Third-party section** — short, because the only third parties are Microsoft (DPA) and optionally a long-tail vendor; this contrast with the vendor route (full 4.1–4.3 due diligence, audit rights, drift monitoring of vendor updates) should be made explicit in the comparison.
