# Evaluating a label-free loss run extraction POC + the WC loss run domain brief

Scope note: Part A covers how to prove extraction accuracy with no labelled data (ground-truth bootstrapping, metrics, realistic thresholds, tooling, bake-off protocol). Part B covers the workers' compensation (WC) loss run domain: canonical schema and header aliases, code sets, California specifics, reconciliation rules, and derived underwriting metrics. All thresholds proposed below are labelled as recommendations and tied to the cited evidence. Research was done 2026-10-21; the WebSearch budget was exhausted partway through, so a few items (noted in Gaps) rely on URLs that could not be fetched.

## A1. Bootstrapping ground truth without labels

### Takeaway
There is no single "free" ground truth; the defensible approach is a layered one: (1) use the ~50,000 previously keyed records as noisy silver labels and align them to documents via claim number + DOI + policy; (2) build a small gold set (hundreds of documents, a few thousand field values) by LLM pre-labelling plus human correction, sized by the binomial/Agresti-Coull formulas; (3) use inter-model disagreement, arithmetic reconciliation (paid + outstanding = incurred, rows sum to totals), and synthetic documents with known truth as cheap, unlimited "weak" checks. Published work shows LLM-only silver labels are unreliable as ground truth (MAE ~15.7% in one study) but model-disagreement scores track true error within ~2% MAE, and clustering (many fields per document) must be corrected for in confidence intervals.

### Cited Findings

**Using previously keyed data as noisy labels / record alignment**
- Record-linkage literature frames accuracy as false match rate P(U | M̂) and false non-match rate 1 − P(M | Û); truth typically comes from "several levels of clerical review, adjudication and field follow-up"; "the minimal sample sizes of training data must be at least 500 pairs"; an unsupervised method treats pairs above a high score as pseudo-true matches and below a low score as pseudo-true non-matches, then runs semi-supervised EM; it warns estimating false non-match rates is often "(nearly) impossible" without auxiliary information — [US Census Bureau RRS2007-05](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2007/adrm/rrs2007-05.pdf)
- A 2025 paper estimates the false discovery rate of record linkage by injecting synthetic records (set at 10% of file size) that should never match; any link they form is a false positive; naive model-probability FDR estimates "tend to underestimate the FDR" — [Robach, Hof & van de Wiel, "False Discovery estimation in Record Linkage"](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.20627)
- Annotation error rates "are usually estimated by manually inspecting a subsample" but samples are often too small; inspection is sampling without replacement (hypergeometric); binomial approximation acceptable only when dataset ≥10× sample; recommends exact Clopper-Pearson intervals over Wald; with a 5% true error rate, 100 samples give 5±4% and 200 samples give 5±2.75%; acceptance sampling "can reduce the required sample sizes up to 50% while providing the same statistical guarantees" — [Klie et al., "On Efficient and Statistical Quality Estimation for Data Annotation" (arXiv 2405.11919)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11919)
- A knowledge-graph-informed council of three LLM agents flagged 488 of 500 known label errors (P 1.00 / R 0.98 / F1 0.99) on a balanced 1,000-item subset vs. a single LLM at F1 0.48; the system is designed as conservative error-flagging for human review, not auto-rewriting; weak on "hierarchical" errors (3 of 15 caught); ~94 minutes per 1,000 items — [Adjudicator (arXiv 2512.13704)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13704)

**LLM-as-labeller with human spot-check**
- ExtractBench's ground truth was built "three ways: cross-model agreement on real documents with human review of disagreements, data-first synthetic long lists, and 169 regulatory and tax forms with manual field checks" — [LlamaIndex ExtractBench](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/introducing-extractbench)
- ParseBench recommends "human-verified ground truth with a VLM-assisted pipeline to keep annotation costs manageable" (2,078 human-verified pages from 1,180 documents, 54.5% of table pages from SERFF insurance filings) — [ParseBench (arXiv 2604.08538)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08538)
- Microsoft's Content Understanding calibration guidance: "Collect representative, human-verified production documents"; "Measure accuracy per field"; held-out evaluation used "over 22,000 field values from more than 1,000 production documents"; "Test on held-out documents, since calibration-set results can be overly optimistic"; run an ongoing "outer loop" sampling human-checked results including ones not flagged for review — [Microsoft Foundry blog, Oct 14 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/content-understanding-confidence-grounding-hitl/)
- Content Understanding Studio "Auto label" runs the existing analyzer to prepopulate labels, humans correct, corrected fields get a "corrected" tag; "Labeled samples don't fix OCR errors" — [Azure Content Understanding analyzer improvement](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/document/analyzer-improvement)

**Inter-model agreement (disagreement as proxy for error)**
- Ensemble disagreement scores (models differing only by seed) predict true F1 with ~1.91% MAE on average (up to 9% in some cases), ~2.5% MAE across 10 languages for fine-tuned XLM-R; by contrast using few-shot GPT-4 "silver labels as a stand-in for human labels produced much larger errors, averaging about 15.7% MAE and reaching 31.3%"; "no theoretical error bound holds under domain shift" — [Effective Proxy for Human Labeling: Ensemble Disagreement Scores (GEM 2023)](https://aclanthology.org/2023.gem-1.5.pdf)
- OmniAI's benchmark scored its own ground truth by feeding 100% correct text plus the schema to GPT-4o: "typically yields about 99% accuracy, with a variation of ±1%" — i.e., even perfect input shows ~1% judge noise — [OmniAI OCR Benchmark](https://getomni.ai/ocr-benchmark)

**Sample sizes and confidence intervals**
- Sample size for a proportion: n = Z²·p(1−p)/e²; worst case p = 0.5 at 95%: ±5% → n = 400 (simplified 1/B²), ±3% → "approximates to n = 1000" (1/0.03² ≈ 1,111; Z = 1.96 form ≈ 1,067) — [Wikipedia: Sample size determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_size_determination)
- EDRM e-discovery sampling table: 90%/±5% → 271; 95%/±2.5% → 1,537; 98%/±1% → 13,526; 99%/±0.5% → 66,358; "Sampling a population for a certain error and confidence interval does not depend on the size of the population"; recommends stratified sampling — [EDRM Search Guide Appendix 2](https://edrm.net/resources/project-guides/edrm-search-guide/appendix-2)
- For classifier/LLM accuracy: "Avoid the Wald and basic percentile bootstrap"; "Default to Agresti-Coull" (add 2 successes and 2 failures); Wilson is a reasonable alternative; Clopper-Pearson is exact and conservative; for F1 use a pseudo-count bootstrap (λ = 1); nested data (many texts per individual ≈ many fields per document): design effect DEFF = 1 + ρ(B−1), effective n_eff = n / DEFF; an unadjusted interval with ~30 items per cluster and ICC ≈ 0.11 covered the true 0.90 only ~65% of the time; Table 3 positives needed for 95% CI half-width ≤0.025 / ≤0.05 / ≤0.10: at true performance 0.90 → 547 / 138 / 36; at 0.95 → 297 / 78 / 23; at 0.80 → 978 / 243 / 60 — [Estimating Uncertainty in Classifier Performance... (arXiv 2606.26422)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26422)

**Consistency / reconciliation as weak supervision**
- Statistical-plan edits already encode arithmetic truth: "Paid indemnity cannot be greater than incurred indemnity" (edit 000082); "Paid medical cannot be greater than incurred medical" (000083); "No duplicate claim numbers allowed" (000066); "Total claim amount for loss > $2,000 must have a claim number" (000077); incurred indemnity must be zero for injury code 6 or 7 (000069) — [NCRB USR Edits (Mar 2021)](https://www.ncrb.org/Portals/0/ncrb/workers%20comp%20services/Data%20Reporting/USR%20Edits%20as%20of%20032021.pdf)
- Loss-run data model: "Each row in a lossrun should correspond to a specific claim at a specific point in time"; claim counts come from row counts so each claim must appear once per evaluation date; "Missing values are hidden" when sums drop NAs; derived fields such as report_lag "need checking" against source dates — [lossrx: Lossruns and Triangles](https://jimbrig.r-universe.dev/lossrx/doc/Lossruns-and-Triangles.html)
- Reported (incurred) losses "are always greater than or equal to paid losses" — [SIGMA Actuarial: Understanding Loss Development Factors](http://www.sigmaactuary.com/2011/10/03/understanding-loss-development-factors/)

**Synthetic loss run generation**
- RealDocBench: "Medical and some tax documents are real blank form templates filled with synthetic persona data, so their gold answers are known by construction" — [RealDocBench (arXiv 2606.07401)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07401)
- OmniAI's 1,000-document set is "a mix of annotated and synthetic data... Some synthetic documents had scan-like effects applied, or were printed and photographed" — [OmniAI OCR Benchmark](https://getomni.ai/ocr-benchmark)
- AWS SEED pipeline: data generator → data critic (schema/domain rules) → HTML/CSS doc generator → PDF → vision-model doc critic; saves ground-truth JSON, HTML source and PDF; optional `--augment` applies "aging effects through augraphy" to simulate scanning/faxing artifacts; outputs CSV/Parquet/Excel/JSON for tabular data; "Every artifact is entirely fictional" — [AWS SEED](https://awslabs.github.io/synthetically_engineered_evaluation_data/)
- docsynth (alpha, MIT): "generates synthetic documents and, alongside them, a computed golden dataset of the true values for every field"; golden values deterministic and exact (Decimal survives Parquet/SQL round trips); evaluation is "a SQL join between golden and extracted tables"; only an invoice pack ships today; PDF via Playwright/Chromium; no XLSX/image output documented — [docsynth on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/docsynth/)
- ExtractBench includes "data-first synthetic long lists" and a "Repeated records (up to 26,725 rows, 17 docs)" segment where the best system scores 94.4% and "most other systems fall much lower" — [ExtractBench](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/introducing-extractbench)

### Inferences
- Alignment key for the 50k archive: (normalized claim number, DOI, policy number/period, carrier) is the natural composite key; expect keyed data to be a *valuation snapshot* that may differ legitimately from the document if the keyer used a different loss run date — so the "noisy label" error has two components (keying error and valuation-date mismatch). Only documents whose stated valuation date matches the keyed snapshot date should be used as labels for dollar fields; claim #, DOI, nature/cause/body and policy period are stable across valuations and can be used from all aligned records.
- Treat disagreement between (a) extraction, (b) keyed record and (c) a second extraction path as the triage signal; adjudicate only disagreements by human (Adjudicator-style), which is where ExtractBench's "cross-model agreement + human review of disagreements" method lands.
- Because the gold set will have many fields per document, report CIs with the design-effect correction (ICC-adjusted n_eff) or a document-level cluster bootstrap; a naïve ±3% claim from 1,067 *fields* is not valid if they come from 100 documents.
- Sample-size arithmetic (computed from the cited formula, not from a source): at an expected accuracy of 95%, n = 1.96²·0.0475/e² gives ≈203 field values for ±3%, ≈456 for ±2%, ≈1,825 for ±1%; at 98% expected accuracy ≈188 for ±2% and ≈753 for ±1%. At p = 0.5 (worst case) the numbers are 1,067 (±3%), 2,401 (±2%), 384 (±5%). Use the worst case for planning when prior accuracy is unknown, then shrink with Agresti-Coull once a pilot estimate exists.
- Synthetic loss runs are good for *coverage* of edge cases (reopened claims, multiple policy numbers, non-CA losses, mixed lines, catastrophic outliers) and for stress-testing arithmetic reconciliation; they are weak evidence for real-world layout/scan variability, so report synthetic results separately and never blend them into the headline accuracy number.

### Gaps
- No published study specifically on using previously keyed insurance submission data as noisy labels for document extraction; the record-linkage and annotation-QA literature above is the closest analogue.
- No source quantifies typical human keying error rates for loss run entry into policy systems; this must be measured on the pilot (e.g., 200-document double-keyed sample).

## A2. Metrics and realistic, defensible accuracy thresholds

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 benchmark picture is consistent: on mixed real-world business documents the best extraction systems score roughly 90–96% per field (RealDocBench per-field 86–96% for top systems; ExtractBench value-F1 87–96%; OmniAI JSON accuracy 85–92%), with per-document "all fields right" rates 5–17 points lower; born-digital PDFs and spreadsheets score materially higher than scans, and tables/multi-column layouts are the dominant failure mode. For loss runs (tabular, numeric, mostly born-digital or clean scans) realistic targets are ≥98% normalized match on Claim # and DOI for native inputs and ≥95% on scans, ≥97%/≥93% on dollar fields, with a document-level straight-through rate of 65–85% depending on source control, and classification precision/recall ≥95%.

### Cited Findings

**Published benchmark levels (2025–2026)**
- RealDocBench (581 single-page regulated documents, 3,742 keys; mortgage/finance/supply chain/medical): per-field / per-question accuracy — Extend Performance v2 96.0% / 90.9%; Claude Opus 4.8 93.0% / 87.1%; LlamaParse Agentic 92.2% / 84.5%; Reducto Agentic 91.4% / 83.8%; best open-source (Chandra-2) 86.2% / 78.1%; AWS Textract 70.7% / 54.0%; Azure DI ~89.1% per-field at ~6.5 s/page; "Per-question scores drop 5–17 points below per-field scores"; "Table and multi-column questions are a major failure area"; mortgage docs easiest (92–98%), medical hardest (best 89.8%); numbers compared after normalization ("$1,234" equals 1234), dates typed YYYY-MM-DD, short strings exact normalized equality, long strings fuzzy ≥92 — [RealDocBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07401)
- ExtractBench (370 enterprise documents, 4,869 pages, 67 document types): value-level F1 — LlamaExtract Agentic Plus 95.6%; Codex (GPT-5.5) 93.6%; Reducto Deep Extract 90.4%; LlamaExtract Agentic 89.5%; Claude Code (Opus 4.8) 87.1%; Extend 86.3%; Gemini 3.5 Flash 79.8%; GPT-5.4 Nano 74.9%; short docs (≤10 pages) "eight of 14 systems score above 90%"; perception conditions tagged born-digital/scanned/handwritten/rotated — [ExtractBench](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/introducing-extractbench)
- OmniAI benchmark (1,000 pages incl. tables, handwriting, photos, low-quality scans): JSON accuracy OmniAI 91.7%, Gemini 2.0 Flash 86.1%, Azure 85.1%, GPT-4o 75.5%, AWS Textract 74.3%, Mistral OCR 72.2%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 69.3%, Google Document AI 67.8%, Unstructured 50.8%; formula = 1 − (JSON differences / total ground-truth fields); array value changes count as two errors; results page updated May 2026 — [OmniAI OCR Benchmark](https://getomni.ai/ocr-benchmark); [leaderboard](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/omniai_ocr/); dataset of 1,000 rows with `json_schema`, `true_json_output`, quality labels PHOTO/CLEAN/LOW_QUALITY/HIGH_QUALITY — [HF dataset getomni-ai/ocr-benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/getomni-ai/ocr-benchmark)
- ParseBench (insurance/finance/government pages): overall LlamaParse Agentic 84.9, Cost Effective 71.9, Gemini 3 Flash 71.0, Reducto 67.8; tables: LlamaParse Agentic 90.7, Gemini 3 Flash 89.9; charts: Azure DI 1.6, Textract 6.0; recommends "Score semantic correctness rather than text similarity", "Report dimensions separately and also as a joint pass rate", record-based table scoring (TableRecordMatch keyed by column headers) — [ParseBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08538)
- Extend's LongArray-Extract (45 PDFs, arrays up to 2,205 rows; vendor-run): Extend MAX 99.2%, Reducto Deep Extract 97.4%, Reducto Standard 80.9%, Pulse 64.5–68.8%, LlamaParse 34.7–47.2% (with failed runs scored zero); Extend Light Parse 90.5% field-level on RealDoc-Bench — [Extend accuracy rankings (Aug/Oct 2026)](https://www.extend.ai/resources/document-extraction-api-accuracy-rankings)
- Reducto-cited LongExtractBench (225 long documents, ~358 pages avg, key-based row matching): Reducto 99.6% precision/recall, LlamaExtract 80.0%/77.5% with 22 failures (vendor-authored page, Sep 2026) — [Reducto parser comparison](https://llms.reducto.ai/document-parser-comparison)
- DUDE benchmark (≈5,000 multi-page documents, 1860–2022, born-digital and scanned): ANLS = 1 − LD / max(1,|G|,|P|) with τ = 0.5 threshold; list answers scored with Hungarian matching; "None" answers 12.74%; ECE calibration counts a prediction correct if ANLS > 0.5; AURC for selective prediction; human baseline 74.76 vs best model ~46 ANLS — [DUDE (arXiv 2305.08455v3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08455v3)

**Native vs scan vs spreadsheet and STP levels**
- Vendor-aggregated field-level accuracy ranges (DigiParser; marketing page with inconsistencies): Digital PDF 97–99.5%; bank statements 95–99%; invoices 91–97%; handwritten 62–85%; scans below 150 DPI penalized 12–25 points; "Mixed real-world pipeline: 91–96% before human review" — [DigiParser statistics](https://digiparser.com/statistics/ocr-accuracy-by-document-type)
- "Anything below 300 DPI causes measurable accuracy degradation"; skew of 3–5° hurts table row alignment; straight-through processing "85–92% for controlled sources and 65–80% for uncontrolled sources, rising to 90–95% with tuning"; measure accuracy "on the fields that feed your systems" — [LlamaIndex OCR automation guide](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/ocr-automation)
- IDP vendor-evaluation guide: targets 70–95% automation (STP) for common document types; "Measure field-level accuracy rather than document-level success rates"; "Poor exception handling means 80% automation simply shifts work to different manual queues"; cites 95%+ accuracy as achievable — [idp-software.com vendor evaluation](https://idp-software.com/guides/idp-vendor-evaluation/)
- An OCR survey reports Google Vision ~94%, DocTR ~91%, Tesseract v4 ~85% on scanned FUNSD/SROIE-type sets and treats born-digital PDFs with text layers (pdfplumber/PyMuPDF) as ~100% text extraction — [arXiv 2506.11156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11156)
- Microsoft customer result (insurance): "97%+ page-level classification accuracy", "nearly 50% reduction in human review burden"; targeted review "selected 41% fewer field values while still catching more than 80% of known extraction errors"; confidence "didn't reliably separate true blanks from misses" so empty fields need a separate policy — [Microsoft Foundry blog Oct 2026](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/content-understanding-confidence-grounding-hitl/)
- Illustrative confidence thresholds by field criticality: critical (TotalAmount) ≥0.90, important (InvoiceNumber) ≥0.80, non-critical ≥0.70, "Determine thresholds experimentally" — [Azure Content Understanding best practices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/concepts/best-practices)

**Metric definitions to adopt**
- Per-field accuracy over all keys, per-question (all keys in a group correct), typed normalization for numbers/dates/booleans, fuzzy only for long strings — [RealDocBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.07401)
- Record-level table scoring keyed by headers, insensitive to row order, penalizing dropped/transposed headers (TableRecordMatch); joint pass rate alongside component scores — [ParseBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08538)
- Precision/recall/F1 at value level with grounding (bounding-box) scores at word and page level — [ExtractBench](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/introducing-extractbench)
- Agresti-Coull / Wilson intervals for accuracy, pseudo-count bootstrap for F1, cluster adjustment for nested fields — [arXiv 2606.26422](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26422)

### Inferences
- Recommended threshold set for the acceptance criteria (recommendations, with evidence basis):
  - Claim # and DOI, normalized exact match: ≥98% on native PDF/XLSX, ≥95% on scans ≥300 DPI, ≥90% on scans <200 DPI or faxes. Basis: mortgage-form per-field 92–98% and digital-PDF 97–99.5% figures; loss runs are simpler than mortgage packets (fixed tabular layout, short alphanumeric keys).
  - Indemnity/medical paid and reserved (per claim): ≥97% native, ≥93% scans; incurred may be derived and should be checked against stated totals rather than extracted independently. Basis: numeric cells in tables are the dominant failure area in RealDocBench/ParseBench; the arithmetic reconciliation (A1) recovers most residual errors.
  - Nature/cause/body: ≥95% when the source prints WCIO codes; ≥85% top-1 WCIO mapping when the source is free text, with a documented "NOC/unmapped" bucket — basis: code tables are closed sets but carrier free text is inconsistent (see B2).
  - Claim-level completeness (all required fields present and reconciled): ≥90% native, ≥80% scans — basis: per-question rates run 5–17 points below per-field.
  - Document-level straight-through (no human touch): 80–90% for recurring carrier templates, 65–80% for first-seen templates — basis: STP 85–92% controlled / 65–80% uncontrolled.
  - Auto-classification (loss run vs other; WC vs other line; CA vs non-CA): precision ≥97%, recall ≥95% — basis: 97%+ page-level classification observed in an insurance deployment.
  - Data-quality first-pass rate: ≥85% of documents pass all hard rules without edits (target), measured only on documents that are themselves internally consistent (a document whose printed totals do not sum should not count against the extractor).
  - Insight accuracy on derived metrics: claim counts exact; dollar aggregates within $1 or 0.1% of the reference; frequency/severity within ±1% of the reference computed from the gold set.
  - Audit completeness: 100% is achievable by construction (append-only event log keyed by field) and should be verified by a test that replays edits.
- Report every metric three ways: by input type (native PDF / XLSX-CSV / scan-image), by carrier template (seen vs unseen), and overall, each with 95% CIs (Agresti-Coull, cluster-adjusted). This is what makes vendor claims comparable: a vendor's "99%" without input-type stratification and a CI is not apples-to-apples with any of the numbers above.

### Gaps
- No public benchmark exists for loss runs specifically; the closest published analogues are ParseBench's SERFF insurance filings (tables) and RealDocBench's finance/mortgage forms.
- Vendor leaderboards (Extend, Reducto, LlamaIndex, OmniAI) are each run by a vendor who also appears in the results; no independent 2026 benchmark covering all of them under identical scoring was found.
- DigiParser's native-vs-scan ranges cite NIST/Azure/Google/AWS but do not show underlying data; treat as directional only.

## A3. Evaluation frameworks, tooling and a repeatable regression suite

### Takeaway
Open-source harnesses exist for the scoring layer (OmniAI's benchmark repo, ExtractBench/ParseBench methodology, docsynth/SEED for synthetic truth), and Azure AI Foundry provides the run/record/compare layer (`evaluate()` with F1/similarity/custom code evaluators, cloud evaluation runs, evaluator catalog versioning), but none of them ships a loss-run-aware scorer; the build side should implement a small typed field comparator (dates, currency, codes) as a Foundry custom code evaluator and treat the gold set + prompts + mapping tables as versioned artifacts run in CI.

### Cited Findings
- OmniAI benchmark repo: pipeline "Document ⇒ OCR ⇒ Extraction"; "The primary goal is to evaluate JSON extraction from documents"; modified json-diff accuracy = 1 − (difference fields ÷ total fields); Levenshtein text similarity noted to penalize layout differences; supports Azure Document Intelligence, AWS Textract, Google Document AI, Unstructured, GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini, Mistral OCR, open models; local runs save `results/<timestamp>/results.json` — [github.com/getomni-ai/benchmark](https://github.com/getomni-ai/benchmark)
- ParseBench publishes "datasets, metrics, and configurations so others can reproduce and extend the results" and recommends tolerance rules matched to source (exact for labeled values, relative for estimated) — [ParseBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08538)
- Azure AI Foundry built-in evaluators relevant to ground truth: F1 Score (token-overlap harmonic mean vs ground truth), BLEU/GLEU/ROUGE/METEOR, Similarity (AI-assisted), Response Completeness, String Checker (pattern validation), Model Scorer/Labeler, Rubric (weighted LLM-judged criteria), and Custom evaluators; no exact-match evaluator is listed — [Foundry built-in evaluators](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/concepts/built-in-evaluators)
- Custom evaluators: code-based Python `grade(sample, item) -> float` (sandboxed, 2 min/call, no network, 256 KB code), prompt-based LLM judges returning `{"result","reason"}`, endpoint-based (30 s timeout); referenced in `testing_criteria` alongside built-ins; runs retrieved via `client.evals.runs.retrieve`, output items listed, `run.report_url` — [Foundry custom evaluators](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-my/Azure/foundry/concepts/evaluation-evaluators/custom-evaluators)
- SDK pattern: JSONL rows with `query`, `context`, `response`, `ground_truth`; `evaluate(data="data.jsonl", evaluators={...}, evaluator_config={"f1_score": {"column_mapping": {"response": "${data.response}", "ground_truth": "${data.ground_truth}"}}}, azure_ai_project=..., output_path=...)` logs to the Foundry project and returns `result.studio_url` — [Evaluate SDK](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-studio/how-to/develop/evaluate-sdk)
- Cloud evaluation: `project_client.datasets.upload_file(name, version, file_path="*.jsonl")`, evaluators configured with `EvaluatorConfiguration(id=..., data_mapping={...})`, run via `project_client.evaluations.create(...)`; recommended "for large-scale testing, CI/CD integration, and predeployment testing"; custom evaluators registered to a versioned evaluator catalog (`ml_client.evaluators.create_or_update`, fetched by `version`) — [Foundry cloud evaluation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-my/Azure/foundry-classic/how-to/develop/cloud-evaluation)
- Microsoft ISE harness: experiments as JSONL with arbitrary extra fields usable by custom evaluators; JSON config with `defaults` + `evaluators` list mapped to evaluator classes; "keeping this configuration as simple as possible is also important for readability" — [ISE blog: customizable, extensible evaluations](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/ise/customizable-extensible-evaluations-of-genai-application/)
- Content Understanding: per-field confidence 0–1 via `estimateFieldSourceAndConfidence`; grounding returns spans with page number and bounding box; "evaluate extraction quality before and after training"; training supports document analyzers only; 2026-06-01-preview training distills samples into the analyzer — [Analyzer improvement](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/document/analyzer-improvement); schema best practices: detailed field descriptions with alternative labels ("May be labeled as 'Invoice Date', 'Billing Date', or 'Issue Date'"), arrays of objects for repeated rows, `extract` vs `generate` vs `classify` methods, classify "by semantic category, not visual template", define an "Other" category — [CU best practices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/concepts/best-practices)
- Confidence-threshold recalibration is required "when documents, analyzers, schemas, or the underlying LLM change"; "Don't use a single global threshold" — [Microsoft Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/content-understanding-confidence-grounding-hitl/)
- Synthetic truth generators that emit (document, golden JSON) pairs: AWS SEED (PDF + HTML + ground-truth JSON, augraphy aging, Excel/CSV/Parquet tabular output) — [SEED](https://awslabs.github.io/synthetically_engineered_evaluation_data/); docsynth (invoice pack only, deterministic Decimal golden values, SQL-join evaluation) — [docsynth](https://pypi.org/project/docsynth/)

### Inferences
- A minimal, defensible harness for the build side: (1) `golden/` = versioned JSONL of document id → canonical claim rows (+ valuation date, policy periods, printed totals), with provenance tag {keyed-silver, human-gold, synthetic}; (2) a typed comparator as a Foundry code evaluator: dates parsed to ISO, currency parsed to Decimal (strip $ , parentheses → negative), codes compared after WCIO mapping, claim numbers compared after whitespace/leading-zero/punctuation normalization, with per-field pass/fail and per-document joint pass; (3) run per commit of prompts/mapping tables, log to Foundry with the git SHA and model version as run metadata; (4) a dashboard that shows per-field accuracy by input type and template with CIs, plus a drift panel comparing confidence histograms across runs.
- The built-in token-F1/BLEU evaluators are inappropriate for loss runs (a $12,345.00 vs $12,354.00 transposition scores high on token overlap); use them only for free-text injury descriptions.

### Gaps
- Foundry docs fetched do not describe side-by-side run comparison UI or CI examples in detail (they reference separate articles not fetched).
- No open-source loss-run-specific evaluation dataset or scorer was found.

## A4. How bake-offs are judged and a fair protocol that plays to the build side's strengths

### Takeaway
Published guidance converges on: buyer-held, representative, blind test set drawn from the buyer's own documents (never vendor samples), identical documents and schema to every entrant, scoring rules fixed before the test, per-field rather than overall accuracy, STP/exception rate and reviewer time measured alongside accuracy, and traceability to source page as a scored criterion — all of which favour a transparent in-house build that exposes lineage, confidence and edit audit.

### Cited Findings
- "Effective POC testing requires using actual enterprise documents rather than vendor-provided samples"; "Include representative samples across document types, quality levels, and processing scenarios"; "Establish ground truth datasets with manually verified extraction results"; "Use standardized scoring scales and require evidence for capability claims"; weight criteria by organizational priorities; include 3–5 year total cost — [idp-software.com](https://idp-software.com/guides/idp-vendor-evaluation/)
- "Define the scoring rules before the test begins"; example weights 25% critical-field accuracy, 20% line-item extraction, 15% exception workflow, 15% traceability, 15% ERP export fit, 10% implementation effort; guardrails: avoid "using only clean sample files", do not accept "a vendor-defined pass mark", do not treat "overall accuracy as the only metric", do not generalize from "one strong supplier sample"; measure reviewer time per exception and source-level verification back to file and page — [invoicedataextraction.com IDP RFP checklist](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/intelligent-document-processing-rfp-checklist)
- Benchmark design lessons: score failed/timed-out runs as zero and count dropped rows against the score — [Extend rankings](https://www.extend.ai/resources/document-extraction-api-accuracy-rankings); report a joint pass rate not just averaged components — [ParseBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08538); evaluate on held-out documents because calibration-set results are "overly optimistic" — [Microsoft Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/content-understanding-confidence-grounding-hitl/)
- "the best benchmark is your own workload"; include "both high-quality digital PDFs and low-quality scans" and documents "where correct output can be clearly defined" — [LlamaIndex parser guide](https://www.llamaindex.ai/insights/best-llm-document-parser-2025)
- Acceptance sampling (accept/reject a batch at stated producer/consumer risk) needs far fewer inspections than estimating an error rate to tight precision (e.g., ~92 vs 803 samples in one configuration) — [arXiv 2405.11919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11919)
- Content Understanding exposes per-field grounding (page + bounding box) and confidence, enabling reviewers to "jump to the exact passage in the PDF" — [Analyzer improvement](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/content-understanding/document/analyzer-improvement)

### Inferences
- Proposed protocol (recommendation): (1) the insurer's actuarial/UW team holds a sealed test set of ~300–500 documents stratified by input type (native PDF, XLSX/CSV, scan), carrier/TPA template (top-10 carriers plus a long-tail bucket), and edge cases from the requirement list (missing years, partial runs, closed-only, reopened, cat outlier, mixed NAICS, multiple policy numbers, non-CA, non-WC lines); (2) a separate ~100-document "tuning" set is released to all entrants in advance; (3) every entrant receives the same canonical schema and the same scoring script (publish the comparator code); (4) score per field with normalized matching and CIs, joint claim-level pass, document STP, classification P/R, reconciliation pass rate, latency and cost per document, and a traceability score (can a reviewer click from any value to its source cell?); (5) require entrants to return confidence per field so error-coverage-at-review-rate curves can be compared; (6) time-box human review minutes per exception as a scored item.
- Build-side strengths to make scorable: field-level lineage (page, bbox, raw text, normalization applied), full edit audit (user/time/old→new), customizable mapping tables (carrier aliases, WCIO code crosswalks) that can be changed without vendor tickets, and the ability to run the whole regression suite on demand.

### Gaps
- No insurance-specific published bake-off rubric for loss run ingestion was found; the protocol above is synthesized from generic IDP RFP guidance and benchmark methodology.

## B1. Canonical WC loss run schema, header aliases and layouts

### Takeaway
Across carrier, TPA, statutory and agency-system sources the same core appears: policy identifiers and period, valuation ("as of") date, claim number, claimant, DOI, report date, status, injury descriptors (nature/cause/body, often free text), and money in a paid / reserve (outstanding) / incurred triplet split by indemnity, medical and expense, with recoveries/subrogation, per-policy-period subtotals and grand totals with open/closed counts. Header wording varies widely (e.g., "Date of Injury" vs "Date of Loss" vs "Date of Accident"; "Reserves" vs "Outstanding" vs "Case Reserves"; "Incurred" vs "Total Incurred" vs "Paid + Reserve").

- Canonical enumerations to adopt: status ∈ {Open, Closed, Reopened} with a raw_status passthrough; claim_type ∈ {MedicalOnly, Indemnity/LostTime, Unknown} derived from injury-type code when present (06/07 → MedicalOnly; 01/02/03/04/05/09 → Indemnity) else from money (indemnity paid or reserved > 0 → Indemnity); litigation_flag from explicit column, status text ("in litigation", "L"), attorney-fee columns, or ALAE/legal > 0 as a weaker signal (mark provenance); subrogation from recovery columns or recovery-type code 03/04, and keep gross vs net incurred distinct.
- Nature/cause/body mapping strategy: if the document prints 2-digit codes, validate against the WCIO tables; if free text, map to WCIO with an LLM/classifier and always keep the raw text; score mapping accuracy separately from extraction accuracy because the "truth" for free text is itself a judgment (use NOC codes as the allowed fallback).
- Expect a reopened claim to appear as status "Reopened"/"R", or simply as a closed claim in an earlier valuation that is open with non-zero reserves in a later one; the ingestion schema needs (claim_number, valuation_date) as the row key to represent this.

### Gaps
- The IAIABC Claim Type Code (DN74) and Claim Status (DN73) value lists could not be retrieved (IAIABC guides are member-only and the CA guide's Section K/N tables did not come through in fetched text). Commonly cited values (DN74: I indemnity, M medical only, B became medical only, N notification only; DN73: O open, C closed, R reopened) are from prior knowledge and are unverified here.
- NCCI injury type codes 03 (PPD major) and 04 (PPD minor) are referenced in several secondary sources but were not confirmed in a fetched primary NCCI document.

## B3. California specifics: WCIRB/USR conventions, X-Mod data, CT claims, class codes, reforms, how CA underwriters read loss runs

### Takeaway
California runs its own system: WCIRB (not NCCI) classifications (~700, 4-digit, dual-wage construction classes), a 1995 USRP with 18-month first valuation and annual revaluations up to 10 reports (first three used for rating), and an Experience Rating Plan with a 3-year period starting 4 years 9 months before the rating date, a $250 per-claim deduction, size-based primary thresholds ($4,500–$75,000), no weight on excess losses, eligibility threshold ~$9,200–$10,200 in recent years, and special treatment of subrogated, non-compensable, joint and death claims. The dominant 2025–2026 underwriting story is cumulative trauma: ~26% (2024) rising to ~31.6% (2025) of indemnity claims, ~60% filed post-termination, >90% litigated, slower closure, and heavy concentration in Southern California, which is why CA underwriters read loss runs for CT share, litigation, open-claim reserves and post-termination filings before anything else.

### Cited Findings

**USR valuation and reporting conventions**
- First valuation "18 months after the policy inception month"; first-level USR due "two months after the valuation date" (20 months); open claims revalued every 12 months (30, 42, 54 …) until closed or 10 reports; "Typically, only data contained in the first three reports is used for experience rating purposes"; when a claim opens the insurer estimates total value (present and future) for the next USR — [WCIRB experience rating worksheet/USR timeline](https://www.wcirb.com/node/463); electronic-only WCSTAT 250-byte records via CDX; BEEP edit tool; USDQP participation for insurer groups above a threshold — [WCIRB USR page](https://www.wcirb.com/node/513)
- WCIRB validates USRs in two stages (preprocessing WCSTAT compliance edits; relational audit edits after policy matching) — [WCIRB USR validation](https://www.wcirb.com/node/908); USDQP metrics include "Percentage of USRs processed with one or more edit failures that impact experience rating", large policies (≥$250,000 modified pure premium) with no claims, and documentation required for ≥$1,000,000 policies with no claims — [WCIRB USDQP](https://www.wcirb.com/node/9686)
- Systemic misreporting assessment: USRs "materially inaccurate due to a widespread systemic failure or deficiency" causing numerous incorrect mods; minimum $10,000, maximum 0.1% of direct written premium — [WCIRB special assessment](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/special_assessment_for_systemic_misreporting_of_usrs.pdf)

**Experience Rating Plan (ERP) mechanics relevant to loss data**
- Experience period "three (3) years, commencing four (4) years and nine (9) months prior" to the mod date, ending 1 year 9 months prior; only completed policy periods — [WCIRB ERP 2022](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/9-2022_erp-final.pdf); example: 9/1/2023 rating date → experience period 12/1/2018–12/1/2021 — [WCIRB estimator sheet](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/wcirb_experience_modification_estimator_short.pdf)
- Eligibility threshold (expected losses): $9,900 (2021 ERP) — [ERP 2021](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/1-2021_erp-final_0.pdf); $9,200 (2022 ERP edition) — [ERP 2022](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/9-2022_erp-final.pdf); $10,200 (Sept 1 2022 and Sept 1 2023 examples) — [WCIRB eligibility guide](https://www.wcirb.com/guide-to-workers-compensation/experience-rating/eligibility); $10,000 (2019) — [2019 estimator sheet](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/2019_wcirb_experience_modification_estimator_shortcut_sheet_0.pdf); "adjusted annually"

- Primary thresholds since 2017 vary by employer size: "over 90 different primary loss values ranging from $4,500 to $75,000"; primary gets full weight, excess no weight; 2010–2016 the first $7,000 was primary with excess weighted 0–78%; "Starting with 2019 modifications, the first $250 of each claim is removed"; the WCIRB "cannot revise a mod just because a claim's value changes" but may for non-compensable, subrogation reimbursement, ownership change, reclassification — [WCIRB experience modification guide](https://www.wcirb.com/guide-to-workers-compensation/experience-rating/experience-modification); [node/466](https://www.wcirb.com/node/466)
- Claim value for the mod "may be a closed actual loss value or the current paid value plus reserves established for open claims" — [WCIRB 2019 estimator sheet](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/2019_wcirb_experience_modification_estimator_shortcut_sheet_0.pdf)

## B4. Reconciliation and data-quality rules underwriters/actuaries expect

### Takeaway
Most hard rules come straight from statistical-plan edits and actuarial identities: paid ≤ incurred by component; incurred = paid + reserve; medical-only claims carry zero indemnity; closed claims should carry zero reserves (open "future payments are expected"); no duplicate claim numbers within a carrier; DOI not in the future and within a policy period that is on the run; no negative amounts except recoveries; claim counts and category sums must tie to printed subtotals; and reopened claims are detected by status or by reserves reappearing in a later valuation. Softer rules (reserve adequacy, lag thresholds, missing-year detection) are judgment-based but widely used.

### Cited Findings
- NCRB USR edits: "Paid indemnity cannot be greater than incurred indemnity" (000082); "Paid medical cannot be greater than incurred medical" (000083); "No duplicate claim numbers allowed" (000066); claim number invalid characters (000065); "Total claim amount for loss > $2,000 must have a claim number" (000077); open claim from a previous report must appear on the current report (000176); "Incurred indemnity amount must be zero for injury code of 6 or 7" (000069); incurred indemnity cannot be zero when incurred medical > 0 for certain injury codes (000068); status 2 (reopened) not allowed on certain USRs (000185); every loss in a catastrophe must share the accident date (000074); accident date linked to an exposure record with premium > 0 (000062) — [NCRB USR Edits](https://www.ncrb.org/Portals/0/ncrb/workers%20comp%20services/Data%20Reporting/USR%20Edits%20as%20of%20032021.pdf)
- Status definitions: 0 Open "future payments are expected"; 1 Closed "no future payments are expected" — [MA Stat Plan](https://www.wcribma.org/mass/ToolsAndServices/UnderwritingToolsandForms/Manuals/StatPlan/MA_STATPLAN_20240701.pdf); Florida: closed = "Final payment has been made, though the claim may reopen" — [Florida USR](https://www.formsworkflow.com/form/details/8850-unit-statistical-report)
- Kentucky: "No negative amounts may be entered"; dates MM/DD/YYYY; yearly totals required; reserve-minus-minimum column must not be negative; totals must reconcile across the two reports — [Kentucky instructions](https://elc.ky.gov/Workers-Compensation/Workers%20Compensation/Data%20Reporting%20Instructions.pdf)
- New Mexico: exclude subrogation/third-party/excess recoveries from paid losses; column totals by body part and by benefit type "must be equal" — [NM AER](https://workerscomp.nm.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2024_NM_WCA_AER_Instructions.pdf)
- Reported losses "are always greater than or equal to paid losses"; factors below 1.0 occur when "incurred losses develop downward (claims settle for less than reserved)" — [SIGMA Actuarial](http://www.sigmaactuary.com/2011/10/03/understanding-loss-development-factors/)
- Losses develop for three reasons: late-reported IBNR, "case reserves set by claims examiners that are adjusted as facts emerge", and "closed claims that are reopened" — [captive.com](https://www.captive.com/articles/basics-of-loss-development-triangles)
- Subrogated-claim rule for rating: Actual Losses = gross incurred × (Net Incurred / gross incurred); non-compensable claims excluded entirely — [WCIRB ERP 2022](https://www.wcirb.com/sites/default/files/documents/9-2022_erp-final.pdf); subrogation recoveries must be reported timely or mods are wrong — [CDI Peeters decision](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0250-insurers/0500-legal-info/0600-decision-ruling/0100-precedential/upload/Peeter-s-Transportation-Company-Inc.pdf)
- Experience-period completeness: "An experience modification will not be promulgated with partial self-insured data"; revaluation required if closed claims "were later reopened or revalued" — [WCIRB node/517](https://www.wcirb.com/node/517); State Fund: if loss runs can't be included "provide a detailed explanation" — [State Fund](https://www.statefundca.com/broker/how-to-submit-a-quote/loss-run-information/)
- Large-policies-with-no-claims documentation requirement (≥$1,000,000 modified pure premium) — [WCIRB USDQP](https://www.wcirb.com/node/9686)
- Underwriters reconcile "whether closed claims remain open, since incorrect reserves can skew renewal pricing" — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/what-is-a-loss-run-definition-purpose-and-how-underwriters-use-it/); stale valuation dates "should be flagged and updated"; example query flags "reserve increases above $50k in the past 12 months"; "Duplicate or merged claim numbers can distort trend analysis" — [Nomad Data](https://www.nomad-data.com/doc-chat/streamlining-loss-run-report-analysis-for-aggregate-risk-trends-in-workers-compensation-general-liability-construction-and-commercial-auto-a-guide-for-the-underwriting-manager)
- Loss-run data model rules: each claim appears once per evaluation date; NA ≠ 0 in a triangle; coverage must be filtered first because lines have different fields — [lossrx](https://jimbrig.r-universe.dev/lossrx/doc/Lossruns-and-Triangles.html)
- CA Ins. Code §11663.5 requires claims "detailed by date of claim" with total incurred and paid — [§11663.5](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/insurance-code/ins-sect-11663-5/)

### Inferences
- Recommended rule catalog (H = hard/fail, S = soft/warn), each scored for the "data quality validation pass rate" metric:
  - H1 claim_number present when total incurred > $2,000 (statistical-plan convention); H2 claim_number unique within (carrier, policy); S2 duplicate claim numbers across carriers/TPAs allowed but flagged.
  - H3 DOI ≤ valuation_date and DOI ≤ date_reported (when printed); H4 DOI within one of the policy periods on the run (S4 if run lists multiple policy numbers or a CT claim whose DOI falls after the exposure period — LC 5412 can place DOI after employment).
  - H5 per component: paid ≤ incurred (indemnity, medical, expense); H6 incurred = paid + reserve (±$1 rounding) when all three printed; H7 total columns equal sum of components when printed.
  - H8 injury_type/claim_type MedicalOnly ⇒ indemnity paid = indemnity reserve = 0; S8 indemnity > 0 with claim_type MedicalOnly ⇒ flag "converted to indemnity".
  - H9 status Closed ⇒ reserves = 0 (S9 if the carrier is known to show closed claims with residual reserves; warn and route).
  - H10 no negative paid/reserve values except in recovery/subrogation columns or when printed in parentheses as recoveries; S10 net incurred < gross incurred requires a recovery amount.
  - H11 per-policy-period subtotals and grand totals equal sums of extracted rows; H12 printed open/closed/total counts equal extracted counts; H13 "no losses" statement ⇒ zero rows.
  - S14 reopened: status Reopened, or status Closed in an earlier valuation and Open with reserves > 0 later; S15 missing years: gaps between consecutive policy periods on the run vs the years requested; S16 valuation_date older than the carrier's currency window (e.g., 90–100 days); S17 reporting lag > N days; S18 large-loss flag at the carrier's threshold (see B5); S19 jurisdiction ≠ CA; S20 line of business ≠ WC (mixed GL/auto rows); S21 non-WC class codes / NCCI vs WCIRB code format mismatch (4-digit left-padded).
- Pass-rate accounting: a document should count as "failed by the extractor" only when a human confirms the source document is internally consistent; keep a separate "source inconsistent" bucket so the POC is not penalized for carrier errors (and so those errors become underwriting signal).

### Gaps
- WCIRB's own USR audit-edit list was not retrievable; CA-specific edit codes are inferred from NCRB/MA plans, which follow the same WCIO/WCSTAT structure.
- No source gives an industry-standard reporting-lag or reserve-adequacy numeric threshold; the "24 hours" and "$50k reserve increase" figures are single-source practitioner heuristics.

## B5. Derived metrics with precise definitions and how they feed pricing

### Takeaway
Frequency = claim count ÷ exposure (payroll, premium, employees, hours, or expected losses depending on the bureau); severity = average cost per claim (usually incurred per claim, often per lost-time claim); incurred = paid + case reserves; loss ratio = (incurred [+ LAE]) ÷ earned premium with WC targets around 65–70%; development is measured from loss runs at successive valuation dates via age-to-age factors (paid factors run higher than incurred factors) and losses are capped per claim before trending; experience rating consumes primary (capped at the split/primary threshold) vs excess losses, net of subrogation, with medical-only discounted in NCCI states. Published definitions of each metric are collected below; where a term is underwriting jargon without a published definition (closure velocity, paid-to-incurred ratio) that is noted.

### Cited Findings
- "Frequency: The number of claims, normalized for the size of the program. Calculated as claim count divided by exposure"; "Severity: The average cost of each claim"; pure premium/loss cost = frequency × severity; "Ultimate losses: The estimated losses for any given year when all claims have been reported, paid, and closed"; "Trend: A factor applied to losses in a historical year to reflect the same loss experience in 'today's dollars'"; loss triangle = "A method of organizing loss data by year (rows) and age in months (columns)"; cumulative factors "develop losses from their age of development to ultimate"; triangles may be built on limited data "capped at a specified amount, for example $250,000 per claim" — [Milliman glossary](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/a-beginners-guide-to-the-casualty-actuarial-language)
- Frequency denominators in use: WCIRB indemnity claim frequency "17.8 per 1,000 employees in 2025" — [Business Insurance July 2026](https://www.businessinsurance.com/surging-cumulative-trauma-claims-driving-frequency-costs/); PCRB "claim frequency per $1 million expected losses at constant (2011) wages" (28.11 in PY2011 → 15.02 in PY2021) — [PCRB Exhibit 8](https://www.pcrb.com/media/obpkqss4/exhibit-8.pdf); NCCI describes frequency as claim counts (medical-only, indemnity or both) over "a selected exposure base, which could be payroll, premium, number of workers" — [WorkCompCentral/NCCI 2018 (paywalled excerpt)](https://www.workcompcentral.com/news/article/id/afa8aed1fcd297c76366b20e1fb7eab00aa7ceb4); BLS/OSHA incidence rate = (N × 200,000) / EH where 200,000 "represents the equivalent of 100 employees working 40 hours per week, 50 weeks per year"; DART = cases with days away, job transfer or restriction — [BLS incidence rates](https://www.bls.gov/iif/overview/compute-nonfatal-incidence-rates.htm)
- Claims per 100,000 workers and average ultimate cost per case by injury type are the NCCI Annual Statistical Bulletin presentation (Illinois exhibit) — [Illinois DOI HB 2137](https://idoi.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/insurance/reports/reports/hb-2137-responses-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- Recommended metric definitions for the POC's "insight accuracy vs reference set" (each computable from the canonical schema plus payroll/premium inputs; label the denominator explicitly):
  - Claim frequency per $100 payroll = claims ÷ (payroll/100); per $1M payroll = claims × 1,000,000 ÷ payroll; per 100 FTE = claims × 200,000 ÷ hours worked (or claims ÷ (FTE/100)); per policy year = raw counts by policy period; report indemnity-claim frequency separately (the bureau convention) and total-claim frequency.
  - Severity = total incurred ÷ claim count, reported for all claims, indemnity-only claims, and medical-only claims; also median and capped severity (cap at the large-loss threshold used for pricing).
  - Paid-to-incurred ratio = Σ paid ÷ Σ incurred by policy year; a low ratio in old years signals reserve-heavy open claims (possible future adverse development); a ratio of 1.0 with open claims signals under-reserving — interpretation only, no published numeric norm found.
  - Medical-only vs lost-time split = count and incurred share; lost-time share vs the 20–25% national rule of thumb.
  - Large loss / shock loss: flag claims ≥ carrier threshold (common pricing practice caps at e.g. $250k per claim for trend; NCCI "large" = $1M); compute limited and unlimited totals.
  - Loss trend by policy year = frequency and severity series with on-level adjustment left to actuaries; the POC should output raw series plus valuation age (months from policy inception to valuation date) so actuaries can apply LDFs.
  - Litigation rate = litigated claims ÷ indemnity claims (and ÷ all claims); subrogation effectiveness = recoveries ÷ gross incurred on claims with recovery flags.
  - Closure velocity = share of claims closed within 12/18/24 months of DOI (needs date_closed or status by valuation); claim duration = date_closed − DOI for closed claims; reporting lag = date_reported − DOI (median and % > 7/30 days).
  - Development: requires ≥2 valuations of the same claim set; the POC can compute incurred and paid age-to-age factors when the archive holds prior loss runs for the same insured; otherwise emit per-claim (eval_date, age, paid, incurred) rows in the lossrx shape for actuarial use.
  - Experience-rating inputs: per claim Actual Losses (indemnity + medical incurred, net of subrogation), with the $250 deduction and primary threshold applied by the rating engine, plus expected losses from payroll × ELR; the loss run contribution is the claim-level incurred and status at the right valuation.
- "Reference set" for these metrics should be computed from the human-gold documents with the same formulas; agreement should be reported as exact match for counts and within ±0.1% (or $1) for dollar aggregates, since any divergence is a direct consequence of extraction errors and lets the team trace which field drove it.
